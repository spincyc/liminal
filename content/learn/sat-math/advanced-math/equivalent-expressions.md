# Equivalent expressions

Two expressions are equivalent when they give the same value for every
allowed x: (x + 3)² and x² + 6x + 9 are one example. This skill asks you to
expand, factor, combine exponents and simplify fractions without changing
the value. It belongs to Advanced Math, {{fact:sat-math-advanced}} of the
Math section.

## Before you start {#before-you-start}

This page uses the integer exponent rules from Grade 8 and two Algebra
skills: multiplying binomials and factoring a simple trinomial. Try these
first. If one goes wrong, rebuild it with
[lesson 1-6, properties of exponents](lessons.html#common-core-math/8/1-6),
[lesson 1-7, zero and negative exponents](lessons.html#common-core-math/8/1-7),
[Algebra, week 20: multiplying polynomials](weeks.html#high-school-math/algebra/20) or
[Algebra, week 21: factoring quadratics](weeks.html#high-school-math/algebra/21).

> **Try it.** Write x⁵ · x³ ÷ x² as a single power of x.
>
> **Answer.** x⁶
>
> Multiplying adds the exponents and dividing subtracts them:
> 5 + 3 − 2 = 6. Shaky? [Lesson 1-6](lessons.html#common-core-math/8/1-6).

> **Try it.** What is the value of 2⁻³?
>
> **Answer.** 1/8
>
> A negative exponent means a reciprocal: 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8. Shaky?
> [Lesson 1-7](lessons.html#common-core-math/8/1-7).

> **Try it.** Expand (x + 4)(x − 2).
>
> **Answer.** x² + 2x − 8
>
> Multiply every term by every term: x² − 2x + 4x − 8, and
> −2x + 4x = 2x. Shaky? [Algebra, week 20](weeks.html#high-school-math/algebra/20).

> **Try it.** Factor x² + 7x + 12.
>
> **Answer.** (x + 3)(x + 4)
>
> Find two numbers with product 12 and sum 7: 3 and 4. Check:
> x² + 4x + 3x + 12 = x² + 7x + 12. Shaky?
> [Algebra, week 21](weeks.html#high-school-math/algebra/21).

## Factoring {#factoring}

Expanding and factoring are the same skill run in opposite directions.
Expand by multiplying every term by every term; factor by recognizing a
pattern.

To add or subtract polynomials, combine like terms. A minus sign in front of
parentheses changes the sign of every term inside.

> **Try it.** Which expression is equivalent to
> (4x² − 3x + 7) − (x² − 5x − 2)?
>
> **Answer.** 3x² + 2x + 9
>
> The minus sign changes every sign: 4x² − 3x + 7 − x² + 5x + 2. Combine
> like terms: 3x² + 2x + 9. Changing only the first sign gives 3x² − 8x + 5.

To multiply, multiply every term by every term, then combine. A square is a
product too: (x − 1)² = x² − 2x + 1, not x² + 1.

> **Example.** Which expression is equivalent to (2x − 3)(x + 4) − (x − 1)²?
>
> Multiply: (2x − 3)(x + 4) = 2x² + 8x − 3x − 12 = 2x² + 5x − 12.
>
> Square: (x − 1)² = x² − 2x + 1.
>
> Subtract every term: 2x² + 5x − 12 − x² + 2x − 1 = x² + 7x − 13.

> **Try it.** The expression (3x + 2)(x − 5) − (x + 3)² is equivalent to
> ax² + bx + c, where a, b and c are constants. What is the value of b?
>
> **First step.** Multiply: (3x + 2)(x − 5) = 3x² − 15x + 2x − 10 =
> 3x² − 13x − 10. Now expand the square and subtract.
>
> **Answer.** −19
>
> (x + 3)² = x² + 6x + 9. Subtract every term:
> 3x² − 13x − 10 − x² − 6x − 9 = 2x² − 19x − 19, so b = −19.

| Pattern | Factored form |
| --- | --- |
| Common factor | ax + ay = a(x + y) |
| Difference of squares | a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b) |
| Perfect square | a² + 2ab + b² = (a + b)² |
| Perfect square | a² − 2ab + b² = (a − b)² |
| Trinomial | x² + (p + q)x + pq = (x + p)(x + q) |

The difference of squares hides well: 4x² − 9 = (2x + 3)(2x − 3), and
x⁴ − 16 = (x² + 4)(x² − 4) = (x² + 4)(x + 2)(x − 2).

> **Example.** Factor 6x² + x − 12.
>
> Multiply the first and last coefficients: 6 · (−12) = −72. Find two
> numbers with product −72 and sum 1 (the middle coefficient): 9 and −8.
>
> Split the middle term: 6x² + 9x − 8x − 12.
>
> Group: 3x(2x + 3) − 4(2x + 3) = (3x − 4)(2x + 3).
>
> Check by expanding: 6x² + 9x − 8x − 12 = 6x² + x − 12.

> **Try it.** Factor 4x² + 4x − 15.
>
> **First step.** Multiply the first and last coefficients:
> 4 · (−15) = −60. Two numbers with product −60 and sum 4 are 10 and −6.
> Now split the middle term and group.
>
> **Answer.** (2x − 3)(2x + 5)
>
> Split the middle term: 4x² + 10x − 6x − 15.
>
> Group: 2x(2x + 5) − 3(2x + 5) = (2x − 3)(2x + 5).
>
> Check by expanding: 4x² + 10x − 6x − 15 = 4x² + 4x − 15.

With expressions in the answer choices, you can plug in a number: pick x = 3,
evaluate the original, evaluate each choice, and keep the match.

> **Fails when.** Two choices agree at the number you picked. They do more
> often than you'd think at 0, 1 and 2, so use a value like 3 or 5, and test
> survivors with a second value. Also avoid any x that makes a denominator
> zero.

> **Try it.** Which expression is equivalent to 2x² − 7x − 15?
>
> - A) (2x + 3)(x − 5)
> - B) (2x − 3)(x + 5)
> - C) (2x + 5)(x − 3)
> - D) (2x − 5)(x + 3)
>
> **Answer.** A
>
> Plug in x = 3. The original gives 18 − 21 − 15 = −18.
>
> A) (9)(−2) = −18. B) (3)(8) = 24. C) (11)(0) = 0. D) (1)(6) = 6. Only A
> matches.
>
> Expanding confirms it: 2x² − 10x + 3x − 15 = 2x² − 7x − 15.

Factoring also picks a form: factored form shows the x-intercepts, vertex
form the minimum or maximum, and standard form the y-intercept (see
[nonlinear functions](learn:sat-math/advanced-math/nonlinear-functions#quadratic-functions)).
Check that the form you pick is really equivalent.

For a harder version of factoring, where unknown constants must make two
expressions match for every x, see [matching coefficients](#matching-coefficients).

## Exponent rules {#exponent-rules}

| Rule | Example |
| --- | --- |
| xᵃ · xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ | x² · x³ = x⁵ |
| xᵃ ÷ xᵇ = xᵃ⁻ᵇ | x⁷ ÷ x³ = x⁴ |
| (xᵃ)ᵇ = xᵃᵇ | (x³)² = x⁶ |
| (xy)ᵃ = xᵃyᵃ | (2x)³ = 8x³ |
| x⁰ = 1 for x ≠ 0 | 5⁰ = 1 |
| x⁻ᵃ = 1/xᵃ | x⁻² = 1/x² |

Rational exponents turn roots into powers: the n-th root of x is `x^(1/n)`,
and the n-th root of xᵐ is `x^(m/n)`. So √x is `x^(1/2)` and ∛(x²) is
`x^(2/3)`. Converting both ways is tested directly.

> **Example.** For x > 0, write ⁴√(x³) · √x as a single power of x.
>
> Convert each root: ⁴√(x³) = `x^(3/4)` and √x = `x^(1/2)`.
>
> Same base, so add the exponents: 3/4 + 2/4 = 5/4. The product is
> `x^(5/4)`.

> **Try it.** For x > 0, write ∛x · √(x³) as a single power of x.
>
> **First step.** Convert each root: ∛x = `x^(1/3)` and √(x³) = `x^(3/2)`.
> Now combine them.
>
> **Answer.** `x^(11/6)`
>
> Same base, so add the exponents: 1/3 + 3/2 = 2/6 + 9/6 = 11/6.

> **Example.** Simplify `(8x^6)^(2/3)`.
>
> Apply the power to each factor: `8^(2/3)` · `x^(6·2/3)`.
>
> `8^(2/3)` is the cube root of 8, squared: 2² = 4. And 6 · 2/3 = 4.
>
> The result is 4x⁴.

> **Try it.** Simplify `(16x^8)^(3/4)`.
>
> **Answer.** 8x⁶
>
> Apply the power to each factor: `16^(3/4)` · `x^(8·3/4)`.
>
> `16^(3/4)` is the fourth root of 16, cubed: 2³ = 8. And 8 · 3/4 = 6.

> **Trap.** Multiplying exponents when you should add (x² · x³ is x⁵, not
> x⁶), and applying a power to only one factor ((2x)³ is 8x³, not 2x³).

### Rewriting to a common base {#common-base}

Exponent rules only combine powers of the same base. When the bases differ
but are powers of one number (4, 8, 1/2 are powers of 2; 9, 27, 81, 1/3 are
powers of 3), rewrite every base as a power of that number, multiply out
each exponent, and then compare exponents.

> **Example.** Solve 9^(x − 1) = (1/27)^(x − 6).
>
> 9 = 3² and 1/27 = 3^(−3), so the equation is 3^(2(x − 1)) = 3^(−3(x − 6)).
>
> Same base, so the exponents are equal: 2x − 2 = −3x + 18, so 5x = 20 and
> x = 4.
>
> Check: 9³ = 729 and (1/27)^(−2) = 27² = 729.

> **Try it.** Solve 8^(x − 1) = (1/4)^(x − 11).
>
> **First step.** 8 = 2³ and 1/4 = 2^(−2), so the equation is
> 2^(3(x − 1)) = 2^(−2(x − 11)). Now set the exponents equal.
>
> **Answer.** x = 5
>
> 3x − 3 = −2x + 22, so 5x = 25 and x = 5.
>
> Check: 8⁴ = 4,096 and (1/4)^(−6) = 4⁶ = 4,096.

> **Fails when.** A side is a sum, as in 2^x + 8 = 2^(x + 1): exponents can
> be compared only when each side is a single power of the base. It also
> fails when no common base exists, as in 2^x = 5; then use Desmos.

### A value through a known combination {#known-combination}

Some questions give an equation you can't solve for x and y separately, such
as 2x − y = 3, and ask for the value of a power such as 81^x/9^y. Don't try
to find x and y. Rewrite every base as a power of one number; the exponent
turns out to be a multiple of the given combination:
81^x/9^y = 3^(4x)/3^(2y) = 3^(4x − 2y).

> **Example.** If 2x − y = 3, what is the value of 81^x/9^y?
>
> 81^x/9^y = 3^(4x − 2y) = 3^(2(2x − y)) = 3^(2 · 3) = 3⁶ = 729.
>
> Check with a pair that fits, x = 2 and y = 1: 81²/9 = 6,561/9 = 729.

> **Trap.** Using the given value as the exponent. 4x − 2y is 2 times
> 2x − y, so the exponent is 2 · 3 = 6, not 3.

> **Try it.** If 3x + 2y = 2, what is the value of 64^x · 16^y?
>
> **Answer.** 16
>
> 64 = 2⁶ and 16 = 2⁴, so 64^x · 16^y = 2^(6x + 4y) = 2^(2(3x + 2y)) =
> 2^(2 · 2) = 2⁴ = 16.
>
> Check with a pair that fits, x = 0 and y = 1: 64⁰ · 16¹ = 16.

given sum, see [a square that contains the given sum](#square-of-a-sum).

## Rational expressions {#rational-expressions}

A rational expression is a fraction with polynomials on top and bottom.
Simplify by factoring both and cancelling common factors. You can only
cancel factors (things multiplied), never terms (things added).

> **Example.** Simplify (x² − 9)/(x² + 5x + 6).
>
> Factor: (x + 3)(x − 3) over (x + 3)(x + 2).
>
> Cancel the common factor x + 3: the result is (x − 3)/(x + 2).
>
> The original is undefined at x = −3 and x = −2, and those values stay
> excluded even after cancelling.

> **Try it.** Simplify (x² − 16)/(x² − 2x − 8).
>
> **First step.** Factor the top and the bottom: (x + 4)(x − 4) over
> (x − 4)(x + 2). Now cancel.
>
> **Answer.** (x + 4)/(x + 2)
>
> Cancel the common factor x − 4: the result is (x + 4)/(x + 2).
>
> The original is undefined at x = 4 and x = −2, and both stay excluded.

To add or subtract, use a common denominator, exactly as with number
fractions.

> **Example.** Write 3/(x − 1) + 2/(x + 1) as a single fraction.
>
> The common denominator is (x − 1)(x + 1) = x² − 1.
>
> Numerator: 3(x + 1) + 2(x − 1) = 3x + 3 + 2x − 2 = 5x + 1.
>
> The sum is (5x + 1)/(x² − 1).

> **Try it.** Write 4/(x + 2) − 1/(x − 3) as a single fraction.
>
> **Answer.** (3x − 14)/(x² − x − 6)
>
> The common denominator is (x + 2)(x − 3) = x² − x − 6.
>
> Numerator: 4(x − 3) − (x + 2) = 4x − 12 − x − 2 = 3x − 14.
>
> The minus sign applies to all of x + 2. Writing −x + 2 instead gives
> 3x − 10, a common wrong choice.

Some questions rewrite a fraction as a polynomial plus a remainder:
(x² + 5x + 7)/(x + 2) = x + 3 + 1/(x + 2), because (x + 3)(x + 2) =
x² + 5x + 6, which is 1 less than the numerator.

> **Trap.** (x + 6)/6 is not x + 1. The 6 in the numerator is added, not
> multiplied, so it can't cancel.

> **Desmos.** To test whether two expressions are equivalent, graph both, for
> example `y=(x^2-9)/(x^2+5x+6)` and `y=(x-3)/(x+2)`. Equivalent expressions
> draw the same curve. See [Desmos for equivalence](learn:sat/general/desmos#equivalence).

A formula with several letters is solved for one of them the same way as a
linear one (see [rearranging formulas](learn:sat-math/algebra/linear-equations-in-one-variable#rearranging-formulas)):
clear the fractions, get the target alone, and undo a square or a root
last.

> **Example.** The volume of a cylinder is V = πr²h. Solve for r, where
> r > 0.
>
> Divide by πh: r² = V/(πh).
>
> Take the square root, keeping only the positive value because r is a
> length: r = √(V/(πh)).

> **Try it.** The kinetic energy of a moving object is K = (1/2)mv², where m
> is its mass and v is its speed. Solve for v, where v > 0.
>
> **Answer.** v = √(2K/m)
>
> Multiply by 2: 2K = mv². Divide by m: v² = 2K/m.
>
> Take the square root, keeping the positive value because v is a speed:
> v = √(2K/m).

For a harder version of rational expressions, a fraction built from
fractions, see [fractions inside fractions](#complex-fractions).

## Harder versions {#hard}

The hardest questions in this skill ask you to see structure before doing
algebra: constants that make two expressions match for every x, a value
hidden in the square of a given sum, or a fraction built from fractions.

### Matching coefficients {#matching-coefficients}

When two expressions are equal for all x, their x², x and constant terms
are equal. Expand, match one term at a time, and use the term you didn't
need as a check.

> **Example.** For all x, (ax − 4)(2x + b) = 6x² + x − 12, where a and b are
> constants. What is a + b?
>
> Expand the left side: 2ax² + abx − 8x − 4b.
>
> Match the x² terms: 2a = 6, so a = 3. Match the constants: −4b = −12, so
> b = 3.
>
> Confirm the middle term: ab − 8 = 9 − 8 = 1, which matches. So a + b = 6.

> **Try it.** For all x, (2x + a)(bx − 3) = 8x² + 14x − 15, where a and b
> are constants. What is a + b?
>
> **First step.** Expand the left side: 2bx² − 6x + abx − 3a. Now match
> the terms.
>
> **Answer.** 9
>
> Match the x² terms: 2b = 8, so b = 4. Match the constants: −3a = −15, so
> a = 5.
>
> Confirm the middle term: ab − 6 = 20 − 6 = 14, which matches. So
> a + b = 9.

> **Example.** For all x, (ax + 2)(bx + 1) = 6x² + kx + 2, where a + b = 5.
> What are the possible values of k?
>
> Expand: abx² + (a + 2b)x + 2. So ab = 6 and k = a + 2b.
>
> Two numbers with sum 5 and product 6 are 2 and 3, but nothing says which
> is a. a = 2, b = 3 gives k = 8; a = 3, b = 2 gives k = 7.
>
> Both are possible, so k is 7 or 8.

> **Try it.** For all x, (ax + 3)(bx + 1) = 10x² + kx + 3, where a + b = 7.
> What are the possible values of k?
>
> **Answer.** 11 or 17
>
> Expand: abx² + (a + 3b)x + 3. So ab = 10 and k = a + 3b.
>
> Two numbers with sum 7 and product 10 are 2 and 5, in either order.
> a = 2, b = 5 gives k = 2 + 15 = 17; a = 5, b = 2 gives k = 5 + 6 = 11.

### A square that contains the given sum {#square-of-a-sum}

When the given equation is a sum or difference such as x + 1/x = 5, square
it: the square holds the asked expression plus a middle term.
(x + y)² = x² + y² + 2xy, and (x − 1/x)² = x² − 2 + 1/x².

> **Example.** If x + 1/x = 5, what is x² + 1/x²?
>
> Square the given equation: x² + 2 · x · (1/x) + 1/x² = 25, and
> x · (1/x) = 1, so x² + 2 + 1/x² = 25.
>
> x² + 1/x² = 23.

> **Trap.** Squaring term by term. (x + 1/x)² is not x² + 1/x²; the middle
> term 2 is why the answer is 23 and not 25.

> **Try it.** If x − 1/x = 3, what is x² + 1/x²?
>
> **Answer.** 11
>
> Square the given equation: x² − 2 · x · (1/x) + 1/x² = 9, and
> x · (1/x) = 1, so x² − 2 + 1/x² = 9.
>
> x² + 1/x² = 11.

### Fractions inside fractions {#complex-fractions}

When the top or the bottom of a fraction holds fractions of its own,
multiply the top and the bottom by a common denominator of all the small
fractions. That clears them in one step; then factor and cancel as usual.

> **Example.** Simplify (4/p + 4/q)/(1/p² − 1/q²), where p and q are
> positive and p ≠ q.
>
> Multiply the top and the bottom by p²q²: (4pq² + 4p²q)/(q² − p²).
>
> Factor: 4pq(q + p) over (q − p)(q + p), a difference of squares.
>
> Cancel q + p: the expression is 4pq/(q − p).
>
> Check with p = 1 and q = 2: the original is (4 + 2)/(1 − 1/4) =
> 6 ÷ (3/4) = 8, and 4(1)(2)/(2 − 1) = 8.

> **Try it.** Simplify (1/a − 1/b)/(1/a² − 1/b²), where a and b are
> positive and a ≠ b.
>
> **First step.** Multiply the top and the bottom by a²b²:
> (ab² − a²b)/(b² − a²). Now factor and cancel.
>
> **Answer.** ab/(a + b)
>
> Factor: ab(b − a) over (b − a)(b + a), a difference of squares.
>
> Cancel b − a: the expression is ab/(a + b).
>
> Check with a = 1 and b = 2: the original is (1 − 1/2)/(1 − 1/4) =
> (1/2) ÷ (3/4) = 2/3, and (1)(2)/(1 + 2) = 2/3.

When such a fraction is set equal to a number, clearing it leaves an
equation in x and y that fixes only their ratio. Solve for x/y, then check
whether the question asks for x/y or y/x.

> **Try it.** If (1/x + 1/y)/(1/x − 1/y) = 5, where x and y are nonzero and
> x ≠ y, what is the value of x/y?
>
> **Answer.** 2/3
>
> Multiply the top and the bottom by xy: (y + x)/(y − x) = 5.
>
> Clear the fraction: y + x = 5y − 5x, so 6x = 4y and x/y = 4/6 = 2/3.
>
> Check with x = 2 and y = 3: (1/2 + 1/3)/(1/2 − 1/3) = (5/6) ÷ (1/6) = 5.

> **Trap.** Taking reciprocals one term at a time. 1/(1/A + 1/B) is not
> A + B: with A = 3 and B = 6 it is 1/(1/3 + 1/6) = 1/(1/2) = 2, not 9.
> Add the small fractions first; the result is AB/(A + B).

> **Check.** Put numbers into the original and into your answer, as above;
> equivalent expressions agree. Choose numbers that keep every denominator
> nonzero, and try a second pair if two choices both match the first.
