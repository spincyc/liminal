# Percentages

Percent means "per hundred": 35% is 35/100 = 0.35. Percent questions are
part of Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, {{fact:sat-math-psda}} of the
Math section, but percent thinking also shows up in exponential models and
data questions. The arithmetic is easy; the language is slippery.

## Before you start {#before-you-start}

This page uses percent as a rate per hundred
([Grade 6, week 10](weeks.html#common-core-math/6/10)), finding a missing
whole ([Grade 6, week 11](weeks.html#common-core-math/6/11)) and percent as
a multiplier ([Grade 7, week 10](weeks.html#common-core-math/7/10)). Try
these first, and rebuild any that goes wrong.

> **Try it.** Write 8% and 125% as decimals.
>
> **Answer.** 0.08 and 1.25
>
> Percent means per hundred: 8% = 8/100 = 0.08 and 125% = 125/100 = 1.25.
> Shaky? [Grade 6, week 10](weeks.html#common-core-math/6/10).

> **Try it.** What is 15% of 60?
>
> **Answer.** 9
>
> 15% of 60 = 0.15 × 60 = 9. Shaky?
> [Grade 6, week 10](weeks.html#common-core-math/6/10).

> **Try it.** 12 is 40% of what number?
>
> **Answer.** 30
>
> 0.40 × n = 12, so n = 12 ÷ 0.40 = 30. Check: 40% of 30 is 12. Shaky?
> [Grade 6, week 11](weeks.html#common-core-math/6/11).

> **Try it.** A $50 price increases by 10%. What number do you multiply $50
> by to get the new price, and what is the new price?
>
> **Answer.** 1.10; $55
>
> The new price is 100% + 10% = 110% of the old one, so multiply by 1.10:
> 50 × 1.10 = 55. Shaky? [Grade 7, week 10](weeks.html#common-core-math/7/10).

## Percent change {#percent-change}

```
percent change = (new − old) / old × 100
```

The denominator is always the original value, the one before the change.

> **Example.** A price rises from $80 to $92, and later falls from $92 back
> to $80. What are the two percent changes?
>
> Rise: (92 − 80)/80 = 12/80 = 0.15, a 15% increase.
>
> Fall: (80 − 92)/92 = −12/92 ≈ −0.130, about a 13.0% decrease.
>
> Same $12 change, different percents, because the originals differ.

> **Try it.** A hiking club grew from 40 members to 50, and the next year it
> shrank from 50 back to 40. What are the two percent changes?
>
> **First step.** The rise: the change is 50 − 40 = 10 and the original is
> 40, so 10/40 = 0.25, a 25% increase. Now find the fall.
>
> **Answer.** A 25% increase, then a 20% decrease.
>
> The fall: (40 − 50)/50 = −10/50 = −0.20, a 20% decrease. The change is 10
> both times, but the second percent is of 50.

Write every change as a multiplier. It is faster than computing the change
and adding it, and successive changes just multiply.

| Change | Multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| increase by 15% | × 1.15 |
| decrease by 15% | × 0.85 |
| increase by 100% | × 2 |
| increase by 150% | × 2.5 |
| decrease by 40% | × 0.60 |

> **Example.** A price rises 20% and then falls 20%. What is the net change?
>
> 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96, so the final price is 96% of the original: a 4%
> decrease, not zero.

> **Try it.** A town's population fell 25% in one decade and rose 20% in the
> next. What was the percent change over the two decades?
>
> **Answer.** A 10% decrease
>
> 0.75 × 1.20 = 0.90, so the population ended at 90% of where it started: a
> 10% decrease. Adding the percents gives a 5% decrease, which is wrong.

Running a change backward means dividing by the multiplier, not subtracting
the percent.

> **Example.** After a 25% increase, a price is $90. What was it before?
>
> Original × 1.25 = 90, so the original is 90 ÷ 1.25 = $72.
>
> Check: 72 × 1.25 = 90. The trap answer is $67.50, which is 90 minus 25% of
> 90.

> **Try it.** After a 20% discount, a jacket costs $56. What was its price
> before the discount?
>
> **Answer.** $70
>
> Original × 0.80 = 56, so the original is 56 ÷ 0.80 = $70.
>
> Check: 70 × 0.80 = 56. Adding 20% of 56 gives $67.20, the trap answer.

> **Try it.** A store's sales rose 10% from April to May and 20% from May to
> June. June sales were $3,960. What were April sales?
>
> **Answer.** $3,000
>
> The two changes multiply: 1.10 × 1.20 = 1.32. So April × 1.32 = 3,960, and
> April sales were 3,960 ÷ 1.32 = $3,000.
>
> Check: 3,000 × 1.10 = 3,300 and 3,300 × 1.20 = 3,960. Dividing by 1.30,
> as if the percents added, gives about $3,046.

> **Trap.** Percent against percentage points. A rate going from 20% to 25%
> rose 5 percentage points, but it rose 25 percent (5/20 = 0.25).

For a harder version, see
[the change that restores an amount](#restoring-change).

## Percent applications {#percent-applications}

Three basic questions:

| Question | Setup |
| --- | --- |
| What is p% of n? | (p/100) × n |
| a is what percent of b? | (a/b) × 100 |
| a is p% of what number? | a ÷ (p/100) |

Watch the small words:

| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| increased by 30% | new = old × 1.30 |
| decreased to 30% of | new = old × 0.30 |
| 30% more than x | 1.30x |
| 30% less than x | 0.70x |
| x is 30% of y | x = 0.30y |

> **Example.** A $60 jacket is 30% off, and then 8% sales tax is added to
> the sale price. What is the final cost?
>
> Sale price: 60 × 0.70 = 42. With tax: 42 × 1.08 = 45.36.
>
> The final cost is $45.36. The order of the two multipliers doesn't matter:
> 60 × 1.08 × 0.70 is also 45.36.

> **Try it.** A $45 pair of shoes is 20% off, and then 6% sales tax is added
> to the sale price. What is the final cost?
>
> **First step.** Sale price: 45 × 0.80 = 36. Now add the tax.
>
> **Answer.** $38.16
>
> With tax: 36 × 1.06 = 38.16. In one line: 45 × 0.80 × 1.06 = 38.16.

> **Example.** x is 30% of y, and y is 150% of z. What percent of z is x?
>
> x = 0.30y and y = 1.50z, so x = 0.30 × 1.50z = 0.45z.
>
> x is 45% of z.

> **Try it.** p is 60% of q, and q is 120% of r. What percent of r is p?
>
> **Answer.** 72%
>
> p = 0.60q and q = 1.20r, so p = 0.60 × 1.20r = 0.72r.

> **Example.** 30% of A is equal to 20% of B. What percent of A + B is A?
>
> 0.30A = 0.20B, so 3A = 2B and A : B = 2 : 3.
>
> A is 2 of every 5 parts of the total: 2/5 = 40%.
>
> Check with A = 40 and B = 60: 30% of 40 = 12 and 20% of 60 = 12.

> **Try it.** 25% of m is equal to 15% of n, where m and n are positive.
> What percent of m + n is m?
>
> **Answer.** 37.5%
>
> 0.25m = 0.15n, so 5m = 3n and m : n = 3 : 5.
>
> m is 3 of every 8 parts of the total: 3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%.
>
> Check with m = 30 and n = 50: 25% of 30 = 7.5 and 15% of 50 = 7.5. Pairing
> the larger percent with the larger amount (m : n = 5 : 3) gives 62.5%.

When a question gives percents but no starting amount, start with 100: the
arithmetic is clean and the final number reads directly as a percent.

> **Fails when.** The question gives a real amount you need, or part of the
> quantity doesn't scale with the percent, such as a fixed $5 fee added to a
> percent-based price. Then use the actual numbers or a variable.

For a harder version, see
[a share of a changing whole](#share-of-a-changing-whole).

## Harder versions {#hard}

The hardest percent questions give no amounts: they ask for a missing change
in a chain, or for a part whose share and whole both change.

### The change that restores an amount {#restoring-change}

The missing change is a percent of an amount the question never names, so
start with 100 or divide multipliers; never add or subtract the percents.

> **Example.** A store raises a price by 25%. What percent discount on the
> new price brings it back to the original?
>
> Start with 100: after the increase the price is 125.
>
> The discount must remove 25 from 125: 25/125 = 0.20.
>
> A 20% discount, not 25%. Check: 125 × 0.80 = 100.

> **Try it.** From 2022 to 2023, the number of visitors to a museum
> decreased by 60%. By what percent must the number increase from 2023 to
> 2024 to return to the 2022 number?
>
> **First step.** Start with 100 visitors in 2022. After a 60% decrease,
> 2023 has 40. Now find the percent increase from 40 back to 100.
>
> **Answer.** 150%
>
> The increase is 100 − 40 = 60, and 60/40 = 1.5, a 150% increase.
>
> Check: 40 × 2.5 = 100. The trap answer, 60%, gives 40 × 1.6 = 64.

> **Try it.** A store decreased the price of a bicycle by 20%. Later it
> increased the new price by p%. The final price was 8% more than the
> original price. What is the value of p?
>
> **Answer.** 35
>
> The multipliers multiply: 0.80 × (1 + p/100) = 1.08, so
> 1 + p/100 = 1.08 ÷ 0.80 = 1.35 and p = 35.
>
> Check with 100: 100 × 0.80 = 80 and 80 × 1.35 = 108. Adding the percents
> gives p = 28, but 80 × 1.28 = 102.40.

### A share of a changing whole {#share-of-a-changing-whole}

A part is its share times the whole: part = share × whole. When the share
and the whole both change, multiply their multipliers to get the part's.
Going the other way, share = part ÷ whole, so divide the part's multiplier
by the whole's. Or start the whole at 100 and use actual numbers.

> **Example.** A farm's planted land grew by 25% from one year to the next,
> and the share of it planted with corn fell from 40% to 30%. By what
> percent did the land planted with corn change?
>
> Start with 100 acres: 40% of 100 = 40 acres of corn.
>
> Next year: 125 acres, and 30% of 125 = 37.5 acres of corn.
>
> Change: (37.5 − 40)/40 = −0.0625, a 6.25% decrease.
>
> With multipliers: the share was multiplied by 30/40 = 0.75 and the whole
> by 1.25, so the corn land was multiplied by 0.75 × 1.25 = 0.9375.

> **Try it.** From 2020 to 2024, the number of employees at a company
> increased by 20%, and the percent of employees who work remotely rose from
> 25% to 30%. By what percent did the number of employees who work remotely
> increase?
>
> **First step.** Start with 100 employees in 2020: 25% of 100 = 25 work
> remotely. Now find the 2024 numbers.
>
> **Answer.** 44%
>
> In 2024: 120 employees, and 30% of 120 = 36 work remotely.
>
> Change: (36 − 25)/25 = 11/25 = 0.44, a 44% increase.
>
> With multipliers: the share was multiplied by 30/25 = 1.2 and the whole by
> 1.2, so the remote workers were multiplied by 1.2 × 1.2 = 1.44.

> **Trap.** Reading the share's fall from 40% to 30% as the answer. That is
> 10 percentage points, or a 25% decrease in the share, and neither is the
> change in the corn land, because the whole grew.

The other direction: if the farm's land grew 50% while its corn land grew
20%, a 40% share is multiplied by 1.2 ÷ 1.5 = 0.8 and becomes 32%. Check:
40 acres of 100 become 48 of 150, and 48/150 = 32%.

> **Try it.** The number of students at a school increased by 25%, and the
> number of them who bike to school increased by 10%. Before the change, 40%
> of the students biked. What percent of the students bike now?
>
> **Answer.** 35.2%
>
> The share is multiplied by 1.10 ÷ 1.25 = 0.88, so it becomes
> 40% × 0.88 = 35.2%.
>
> Check: 40 bikers of 100 students become 44 of 125, and 44/125 = 0.352.
