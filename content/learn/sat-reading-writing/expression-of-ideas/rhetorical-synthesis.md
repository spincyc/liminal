# Rhetorical Synthesis

Rhetorical Synthesis questions give you a bulleted list of a student's
research notes and a goal, and ask which sentence uses the notes to meet
that goal. They are part of Expression of Ideas, {{fact:sat-rw-expression}}
of the Reading and Writing section, which comes last in each module. Every
choice is usually accurate to the notes, so accuracy never decides the
question: the goal does. Hard versions use two-part goals and choices that
do a different, equally reasonable job.

## Student notes {#student-notes}

The format is always the same: "While researching a topic, a student has
taken the following notes", a list of facts, then "The student wants to ...
Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to
accomplish this goal?"

1. Read the goal first, before the notes.
2. Break the goal into requirements. "Emphasize a difference in materials between the two libraries" means: both libraries, a difference, about materials.
3. Check each choice against each requirement, like a checklist.

> **Example.** While researching a topic, a student has taken the following
> notes:
>
> - Architect Selma Idris designed the Harbor Library, completed in 1998.
> - Architect Paul Wren designed the Crane Street Library, completed in 2004.
> - Both libraries serve small coastal towns.
> - The Harbor Library is built mostly of local granite.
> - The Crane Street Library is built mostly of glass and steel.
>
> The student wants to emphasize a difference in the two libraries' building
> materials. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the
> notes to accomplish this goal?
>
> A) The Harbor Library was completed in 1998, and the Crane Street Library
> was completed in 2004.
>
> B) Both the Harbor Library and the Crane Street Library serve small coastal
> towns.
>
> C) While Selma Idris's Harbor Library is built mostly of local granite, Paul
> Wren's Crane Street Library is built mostly of glass and steel.
>
> D) The Harbor Library, designed by Selma Idris, is built mostly of local
> granite.
>
> Requirements: both libraries, a difference, about materials.
>
> A gives a difference and both libraries, but in dates, not materials. B
> gives a similarity. D covers materials for only one library. C meets all
> three requirements. C is correct.

> **Trap.** The right facts with the wrong framing. A choice that lists both
> materials in two separate sentences, with no word marking a contrast
> ("while", "whereas", "but"), may not "emphasize a difference". Look for the
> relationship the goal names.

## Rhetorical goal {#rhetorical-goal}

Goals come in a handful of types, and each has its own requirements:

| Goal wording | What the sentence must do |
| --- | --- |
| emphasize a similarity or a difference | name both items and make the relationship explicit |
| introduce X to an audience unfamiliar with it | explain what X is, not assume it |
| explain the purpose or benefit of X | give a reason or aim, not just a description |
| present a finding and its significance | give the result and why it matters |
| make a generalization | state something true across the cases, not one case |
| specify a date and place | include both |

> **Example.** While researching a topic, a student has taken the following
> notes:
>
> - A seed bank is a facility that stores seeds at low temperatures so that they stay alive for decades.
> - The Nordgard Seed Vault holds samples of more than 4,000 crop varieties.
> - Stored seeds can be used to replant crops lost to drought, disease or war.
> - Most seeds are dried and then kept at about −18°C.
>
> The student wants to introduce seed banks to an audience unfamiliar with
> them. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the
> notes to accomplish this goal?
>
> A) The Nordgard Seed Vault keeps samples of more than 4,000 crop varieties
> at about −18°C.
>
> B) A seed bank, a facility that stores seeds at low temperatures so they
> stay alive for decades, can supply seeds to replant crops lost to drought,
> disease or war.
>
> C) Before storage, most seeds in a seed bank are dried.
>
> D) Seed banks such as the Nordgard Seed Vault hold many crop varieties.
>
> The requirement for an unfamiliar audience: say what a seed bank is.
>
> A and D name a seed bank and give facts about it, but a reader who doesn't
> know the term still doesn't know what one is. C is a storage detail. B
> defines the term and adds why it matters. B is correct.

Read the goal as the whole grading rule: a sentence can be accurate, clear
and interesting and still be wrong because it does a different job.

> **Fails when.** A choice meets the goal by adding something the notes don't
> say ("seed banks are the most important tool in modern farming"). The
> answer must meet the goal using only the notes, so check each claim in the
> choice against the list.

> **Note.** Style is not graded here. A slightly awkward sentence that meets
> the goal beats a smooth one that doesn't, and the longer choice is not
> favored or disfavored; many correct choices are long because the goal has
> two parts.

### Emphasize one part, indicate another {#emphasize-while-indicating}

Some goals name two parts and rank them: "emphasize the study's finding
while indicating how the study was conducted". Then:

1. Cross out choices that miss either part, or that swap one for a third thing, such as what the finding suggests.
2. In each remaining choice, find the main clause: the subject and verb that could stand alone as a sentence. The part to emphasize belongs there.
3. The part to indicate belongs in a phrase or a subordinate clause: "By comparing ...", "Having tested ...", ", finding that ...", ", which suggests ...", "since ...".

> **Example.** While researching a topic, a student has taken the following
> notes:
>
> - Engineer Owen Sayre studied traffic noise at homes beside a highway.
> - He wanted to know whether a row of pine trees blocks noise as well as a concrete wall.
> - For six months, he measured noise at 40 homes behind a row of pines and 40 homes behind a concrete wall.
> - Homes behind the pines were nearly as quiet as homes behind the wall.
> - The finding suggests that towns could plant trees instead of building walls in some places.
>
> The student wants to emphasize the study's finding while indicating how
> the study was conducted. Which choice most effectively uses relevant
> information from the notes to accomplish this goal?
>
> A) Sayre measured noise for six months at 80 homes behind pines or a
> concrete wall, finding that the pines worked nearly as well as the wall.
>
> B) By measuring noise for six months at 80 homes behind pines or a
> concrete wall, Sayre found that the pines worked nearly as well as the
> wall.
>
> C) Homes behind a row of pines were nearly as quiet as homes behind a
> concrete wall, which suggests that towns could sometimes plant trees
> instead of building walls.
>
> D) Towns could sometimes plant trees instead of building walls, since
> homes behind pines were nearly as quiet as homes behind a concrete wall.
>
> C and D swap the method for what the finding suggests; D also puts the
> suggestion in its main clause. A and B hold the same facts. A's main
> clause is the method ("Sayre measured noise"), with the finding in a
> phrase. B's main clause is the finding ("Sayre found that the pines
> worked"), with the method in the "By measuring" phrase. B is correct.

> **Trap.** The sentence with both parts in the wrong order of importance.
> Two choices often hold the same facts; check which one the main clause
> carries before choosing.

> **Fails when.** A choice joins two full clauses with "and" or a
> semicolon. Both parts are then stressed equally, so neither is
> emphasized; prefer a choice that tucks the lesser part into a phrase, if
> one does.

## What Hard looks like {#hard}

- Two-part goals ("present the finding and explain its significance"), where each wrong choice does one part.
- Goals that rank two parts ("emphasize the finding while indicating the method"), where two choices hold the same facts and only the main clause decides (see [emphasize one part, indicate another](#emphasize-while-indicating)).
- Goals about audience ("an audience familiar with the research"), where defining a term is unnecessary and the key uses the technical name.
- Choices that each accomplish a real goal, just not this one: describing the method when the goal is the result, or giving a date when the goal is a place.
