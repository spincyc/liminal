# SAT Math plan from around 500

This plan is for a student whose real SAT Math score, or official practice
test score, is around 500 and who wants to improve. Use your official practice
results to identify content gaps, process errors and pacing problems; a score
alone cannot tell you which is responsible. This plan starts with Algebra and
Advanced Math because they make up most of the section, uses practice targets
to organize work, and checks transfer with official practice tests.

## Where the points are {#where-the-points-are}

- Algebra is {{fact:sat-math-algebra}} and Advanced Math is {{fact:sat-math-advanced}} of Math. Together that is about 70% of the section.
- Your Module 1 performance decides whether you get the harder or the easier Module 2 (see [Module 1 decides the route](learn:sat/general/modules-and-pacing#module-1)). College Board doesn't publish the rule. Module 1 mixes easy, medium and hard questions. Use your own misses to choose accessible skills to improve; a score around 500 does not identify the difficulty of every question you can solve.
- Questions are not all worth the same. College Board says two students with the same number right can earn different scores, depending on which questions they got right, including how hard those were (see [how scores are calculated](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/scores/what-scores-mean/how-scores-calculated)). Build reliable methods on accessible questions before spending most of your time on the hardest work. That is the reason this plan introduces Medium practice before Hard, and opens Hard once Medium is routine; Liminal's labels do not establish official item weights or routing effects.

## The sequence {#sequence}

The skills come in four stages, and each skill has a Learn page with the
method and worked examples. You work two stages at a time: Track A runs
stages 1 and 2, Track B runs stages 3 and 4. So Advanced Math starts in the
first week, beside Algebra; together they are most of the section.

If your misses involve axes, scales, endpoints or which coordinate to report,
work through [Reading graphs](learn:sat/general/reading-graphs) alongside
the first stage. Those skills recur in lines, curves and data displays.

1. Linear equations and functions, with percents and ratios: [linear equations in one variable](learn:sat-math/algebra/linear-equations-in-one-variable), [linear functions](learn:sat-math/algebra/linear-functions), [linear equations in two variables](learn:sat-math/algebra/linear-equations-in-two-variables), [percentages](learn:sat-math/problem-solving-and-data-analysis/percentages), [ratios, rates, and units](learn:sat-math/problem-solving-and-data-analysis/ratios-rates-and-units).
2. Systems, inequalities and models: [systems of two linear equations](learn:sat-math/algebra/systems-of-two-linear-equations), [linear inequalities](learn:sat-math/algebra/linear-inequalities), [two-variable data](learn:sat-math/problem-solving-and-data-analysis/two-variable-data), [one-variable data](learn:sat-math/problem-solving-and-data-analysis/one-variable-data).
3. Equivalent expressions, quadratics and exponentials: [equivalent expressions](learn:sat-math/advanced-math/equivalent-expressions), [nonlinear equations](learn:sat-math/advanced-math/nonlinear-equations), [nonlinear functions](learn:sat-math/advanced-math/nonlinear-functions), [systems of equations](learn:sat-math/advanced-math/systems-of-equations).
4. The rest, which is smaller but often quick to learn: [probability](learn:sat-math/problem-solving-and-data-analysis/probability), [statistical inference](learn:sat-math/problem-solving-and-data-analysis/statistical-inference), and the four Geometry and Trigonometry skills, starting with [right triangles and trigonometry](learn:sat-math/geometry-and-trigonometry/right-triangles-and-trigonometry) and [circles](learn:sat-math/geometry-and-trigonometry/circles).

Use Desmos from the first week, not as a last-minute trick. Do the one-week
[Desmos practice plan](learn:sat/general/desmos#practice-protocol) alongside
stage 1, then graph to solve or check in every later stage. Liminal's next
step offers a Desmos drill of about 20 minutes once a week until you have
done four, then every 14 days: ten questions where Desmos is a real route,
most of them systems, nonlinear equations and functions, lines,
inequalities, circles and scatterplots, with the route shown after each.

For each skill, the loop is:

1. Read the Learn page and work its examples by hand, covering the solution first. Until a skill's first drill, the next step offers its lesson beside the drill.
2. Practice the skill at Easy until the method is routine: at least 8 of your last 10 Easy or Medium questions in the skill right, each level counted on its own (8 of your last 10 Easy, or 8 of your last 10 Medium), each question counted once, with no hints.
3. Practice it at Medium, with feedback after each question. Once at least 6 of your last 10 Medium questions are right, about 3 of every 10 drill questions are Hard, so harder versions become routine before the real test.
4. Log every miss in your [error log](learn:sat/general/error-log) with its type and a rule.
5. Move on at the practice target below, or when the skill's budget is used up. It comes back later.

### How Liminal picks the next step {#next-step}

- **One skill in flight on each track.** It is the track's first skill, in the order above, that has not met the practice target and has not had its turn yet. Drills alternate between the tracks, the one you drilled less recently first.
- **A budget per skill.** A skill leaves flight when it meets the practice target, or after 20 questions in its own drills, at any level, since it came into flight: two drills. The diagnostic, mixed sets, timed modules and the Desmos drill do not use it up. A skill that uses its budget first has moved on: it stays in mixed sets and Review, and Progress asks you to see where you stand on about ten official Medium questions. If its drills never got past Easy, Progress flags it too, and its lesson and prerequisite checks are worth rereading before it comes back.
- **A second pass.** When both tracks have been through every skill, the skills that moved on come back into flight in the same order, each with a fresh budget.
- **The diagnostic can start you later.** A stage is passed over while each of its domains went well on the diagnostic (at least 80% of at least 3 questions) and you have not practised its skills. Practising a passed-over skill brings it back, and the rest return in the second pass.
- **A low official check.** A skill whose latest official check is below 60% comes back into flight at the next opening in its track, ahead of skills not yet started.
- **Review first, once a day.** When a missed question is due, the day starts with one Review set of up to 6 questions, most overdue first, misses on skills you have drilled before others. After that the plan moves on, even if more are waiting: Review shows up to 6 for today and how many more wait, your plan keeps moving, and you can do more by choice. A miss on a skill you have not reached in the plan, such as a Hard question in a timed module, is parked: it does not count as due until the skill's first turn, and comes back from that day.
- **Then a rotation.** A timed module of 22 questions in 35 minutes, with answers at the end, once three skills are started (the diagnostic places you but does not count), whenever none was taken in the last 7 days, or after 10 practice sets and at least 3 days since the last one. After a drill, a mixed set: 12 questions from every skill you have started, most from skills in flight or moved on, timed at real-test pace, with answers at the end. The Desmos drill, weekly until you have done four, then every 14 days. Otherwise a drill: 10 questions on a skill in flight, with feedback and a hint after each. "Practice what I missed" counts with Review, not as a drill.

## The practice target {#mastery-gate}

> **Rule.** Liminal's Medium practice target is at least 24 of your last 30
> Medium questions in that skill right, with no hints, and those 30 span at
> least two days and two different question designs. Each question counts
> once, at your first answer: a miss that Review brings back as it was does
> not count again, but a fresh version of it does.

The target no longer holds you on one skill: a skill leaves flight when it
meets the target or uses its budget, whichever comes first. This is a
planning rule, not a statistical demonstration of mastery. Checking
a rolling window repeatedly can produce a passing result by chance, and
familiar question designs can raise accuracy without improving transfer.
Use several question designs across days and check unfamiliar official
questions before treating the skill as secure. All scored Module 1 questions,
not just Medium ones, contribute to the real test's routing and score.
Progress counts practice attempts for you. If you fall short, inspect the
error log for the ideas or steps that need more work.

After a skill passes, the mixed sets and timed modules keep it alive. Hard
questions join a skill's drills once Medium is routine, and lead them once it
meets the target.

Progress shows each skill in one of these states:

| State | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Not started | No answers in the skill yet. |
| Not enough data | Fewer than 5 answers: too few to say anything. |
| Building | Below the target. Follow the next step at Easy or Medium and review misses. |
| Practice target met | At least 24 of your last 30 Medium questions right, over two days and two designs. Add Hard questions. |
| Advanced practice target met | The Medium target, plus at least 10 of your last 15 Hard questions right, over two days and two designs. |

The Hard target calls for more evidence than a few successful answers, but it
still describes practice here. Continue mixed practice and official checks
after meeting it.

> **Note.** The gate is about Liminal's questions, whose difficulty labels
> are Liminal's own. Meeting it suggests a next practice step; it does not
> establish mastery, exam readiness or a future SAT score.

## Official practice tests {#official-practice-tests}

A useful gauge of your score is a fresh full-length official practice test
in Bluebook, scored by College Board (see
[SAT practice tests](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/practice-tests)).
Check Bluebook for the current inventory. Repeating a test can be useful
review, but remembered questions make its score a weaker estimate.

- Take one before you start, as a baseline.
- Then take one every 2 to 3 weeks, under real conditions: using your approved timing and accommodations, in one sitting, with the break, on the device you will use. A 12-week plan calls for about five; adjust to the unfamiliar forms available.
- Take the last one 7 to 10 days before the real SAT, never the day before.
- Record each score in Liminal under Progress, in the Official scores card. It shows each score beside your Liminal accuracy in the 28 days before the test, so you can see whether practice here tracks the real thing. It never turns accuracy into a score.
- Review every miss in My Practice, where College Board shows the questions and your answers, and copy each into your error log. Use the results to choose your next skills.
- Don't take them more often. The learning happens between tests.

## Check Liminal against official questions {#official-questions}

Liminal's Easy, Medium and Hard are its own labels. College Board's
[Student Question Bank](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/student-question-bank),
opened from My Practice, has official questions you can filter by skill and
by difficulty. Use it as a check: after a skill meets the practice target
here, filter the bank to that skill at Medium and do about ten questions you
haven't seen. Record how many you got right under Progress, in Official
question checks; Progress prompts you when a skill reaches the target, and
when a skill's turn ends short of it, to see where you stand. After
the advanced target, check some of its Hard ones too. The latest check of a
skill counts:

- At least 70% right confirms the skill on official questions.
- Below 60%, the skill comes back into flight at the next opening in its track, ahead of skills not yet started, flagged "official check low, keep practising". Trust the official questions.
- In between, nothing changes.

The weekly report on Progress also flags a skill whose latest official
Medium check is 20 or more percentage points below your Liminal Medium
accuracy in it: practice here isn't transferring, so slow down and work its
Learn page and error log before more drills.

> **Fails when.** You judge from two or three questions. A few misses in a
> handful prove little either way; look for a clear gap, such as missing half
> of the official Medium questions in a skill where you miss one in five
> here.

## What Liminal accuracy means {#liminal-accuracy}

Liminal's accuracy is practice feedback on original questions. It is not an
SAT score and can't be converted into one. If your Liminal accuracy is high
but your official practice score is low, trust the official score and look
at three things: your Hard accuracy on its own, whether you practiced with
feedback after every question (which is easier than a real module), and
whether your misses on the official test are content you haven't covered
yet.

## A week in the plan {#week}

- Four or five sessions of 45 to 60 minutes.
- Start the day with Review when something is due: one set of up to 6 missed questions, most overdue first. That is the day's Review; what is still due waits for tomorrow, or for a set you choose.
- Then follow the next step. It alternates drills on the skills in flight, one per track, with a mixed set after each drill.
- At least once a week, a timed module of 22 questions in 35 minutes; the next step offers one after a week, or after 10 practice sets and 3 days.
- About 20 minutes for the Desmos drill when the next step offers it: weekly until you have done four, then every 14 days.
- End with 5 to 10 minutes on your error log.
- When a skill meets its practice target, or its turn ends short of it, check it on about ten official questions and record the result.
- Every 2 to 3 weeks, an official practice test in place of a timed module, with its score recorded under Official scores.
- Once a week, read the weekly report on Progress: it says On track or Needs attention, with the reasons, and you can print it or copy a summary for a parent. Besides missed days and missing modules, it flags practising without learning: a skill that used its turn without becoming reliable at Medium, no skill reaching its target in three weeks of steady practice, and Medium accuracy that is not rising. It reports practice, never a score.

How long a skill takes depends on your answers: it leaves flight at the
practice target or after its budget, so no skill holds the plan for long,
and the ones that moved on come back in the second pass. Stages 1 and 3
usually take the longest, and they are worth it: they cover most of the
section.

## If your score isn't moving {#adjust}

After two official practice tests without a gain, count your error log by
type. Mostly content: slow down and redo the Learn pages for the skills that
keep appearing. Mostly careless: change habits (write every step, circle what
is asked) before adding new content. Mostly time: pacing practice, and check
whether slow questions are really content gaps.
