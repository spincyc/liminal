# Format and scoring

What the digital SAT is, how it is scored, and the rules for entering Math
answers. Every official number on this page comes from College Board and
carries the date it was checked; formats and policies change, so confirm
anything you rely on at the [SAT structure page](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/structure)
before test day.

## The test at a glance {#structure}

| Part | Questions | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading and Writing, Module 1 | {{fact:sat-rw-module-questions}} | {{fact:sat-rw-module-minutes}} minutes |
| Reading and Writing, Module 2 | {{fact:sat-rw-module-questions}} | {{fact:sat-rw-module-minutes}} minutes |
| Break | none | {{fact:sat-break}} |
| Math, Module 1 | {{fact:sat-math-module-questions}} | {{fact:sat-math-module-minutes}} minutes |
| Math, Module 2 | {{fact:sat-math-module-questions}} | {{fact:sat-math-module-minutes}} minutes |

In total that is {{fact:sat-rw-questions}} Reading and Writing questions in
{{fact:sat-rw-minutes}} minutes, {{fact:sat-math-questions}} Math questions
in {{fact:sat-math-minutes}} minutes, and {{fact:sat-total-time}} of testing
time. You take the test in College Board's Bluebook app.

- Each module has {{fact:sat-pretest-per-module}} unscored pretest questions mixed in, so {{fact:sat-rw-scored}} Reading and Writing and {{fact:sat-math-scored}} Math questions count. You can't tell which ones don't, so treat every question as scored.
- Within a module you can move freely, skip, mark questions for review and change answers. Once a module ends, you can't go back to it.
- Reading and Writing: every question has its own short text of {{fact:sat-rw-passage-words}} words (or a pair of texts), and every question has four choices. The domains come in a fixed order: Craft and Structure, Information and Ideas, Standard English Conventions, Expression of Ideas.
- Math: {{fact:sat-math-mc-share}} of questions are multiple choice with four choices and {{fact:sat-math-spr-share}} ask you to enter your own answer. A calculator is allowed on every question, Desmos is built in, and a reference sheet is available. Questions run roughly from easiest to hardest within each module.

## Adaptive modules {#adaptive-modules}

Each section is adaptive between its two modules, not question by question.
What College Board has published:

1. Module 1 has a broad mix of easy, medium and hard questions.
2. Based on your Module 1 performance, Module 2 is either harder or easier on average than Module 1.
3. Your section score comes from all the scored questions in both modules.

Reading and Writing and Math route separately. A rough Reading and Writing
Module 1 doesn't change your Math routing.

> **Note.** College Board has not published the routing rule or exactly how
> the two Module 2 versions affect the score range. It is widely believed
> that the easier Module 2 limits how high a section score can go. Treat
> that as a likely estimate, not a published fact. Either way, Module 1 is
> where your routing is decided, so don't treat it as a warm-up. More in
> [Modules and pacing](learn:sat/general/modules-and-pacing#module-1).

If Module 2 feels much harder than Module 1, that is consistent with the
harder route. You can't know for sure during the test, and it doesn't
change what to do: keep working question by question.

## Scoring {#scoring}

- The total score runs {{fact:sat-total-scale}}; each section runs {{fact:sat-section-scale}}.
- There is no penalty for a wrong answer, so answer every question.
- The score is not a simple count. College Board's model uses which questions you got right, how hard those questions are, and whether your pattern of answers suggests guessing, so two students with the same number correct on different forms can get different scores.
- College Board's [scoring page](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/scores/what-scores-mean/how-scores-calculated) puts it this way: "For most students who are trying their best on every question, it's better to guess than leave a question blank, especially if a student can eliminate one or two answer options before guessing."

> **Note.** Liminal never estimates an SAT score. Its accuracy numbers are
> practice feedback on its own questions, which are original and not
> calibrated against real test takers. The only honest score check is a
> full-length official practice test in Bluebook, scored by College Board
> (see [SAT practice tests](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/practice-tests)).
> Even those scores are estimates.

## Checking against official practice {#official-practice}

Liminal's Easy, Medium and Hard labels are its own judgment of each
question design, made with a written rubric; they are not measured on real
test takers. College Board's free practice is the check on them:

- The full-length practice tests in [Bluebook](https://bluebook.collegeboard.org/), scored by College Board. These are the score gauge.
- The [Student Question Bank](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/student-question-bank): official questions you can filter by section, domain, skill and difficulty. When Progress says a skill has passed its Medium questions here, try that skill's official Medium and Hard questions. If they go much worse, trust the official result and keep working on the skill.
- My Practice, College Board's site where your Bluebook practice-test scores and the Student Question Bank live (start from [SAT practice](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice)).

After each Bluebook practice test, and after a real SAT, record the scores
in Liminal under Progress, in Official scores. Each one sits beside your
Liminal accuracy in the 28 days before it, so you can see whether practice
here tracks the real test. Liminal never converts one into the other.

## Student-produced responses {#student-produced-responses}

For Math questions without choices, you type your answer. The official
rules:

- Up to {{fact:sat-spr-chars-positive}} characters for a positive answer and up to {{fact:sat-spr-chars-negative}} for a negative one (the minus sign counts). Negative answers are allowed.
- If a fraction doesn't fit, enter its decimal equivalent.
- If a decimal doesn't fit, truncate it or round it at the fourth digit.
- Enter a mixed number as an improper fraction or a decimal: 3½ as 7/2 or 3.5.
- Don't type symbols such as a percent sign, comma or dollar sign.
- If more than one answer is correct, enter just one.

| Answer | Accepted | Not accepted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3½ | 7/2, 3.5, 3.50 | 3 1/2, 31/2 |
| 2/3 | 2/3, .6666, .6667, 0.666, 0.667 | .66, .67, 0.66, 0.67 |
| −1/3 | −1/3, −.3333, −0.333 | −.33, −0.33 |

> **Check.** With no choices to compare against, you have no safety net.
> Substitute your answer back into the question, and check that it answers
> what was asked (x or 2x, minutes or hours).

## Reference sheet {#reference-sheet}

Bluebook's Math reference sheet gives:

- Circle: A = πr², C = 2πr. Rectangle: A = lw. Triangle: A = ½bh.
- The Pythagorean theorem, c² = a² + b², and the 30°-60°-90° and 45°-45°-90° special right triangles.
- Volumes: rectangular prism V = lwh, cylinder V = πr²h, sphere V = (4/3)πr³, cone V = (1/3)πr²h, rectangular pyramid V = (1/3)lwh.
- A circle has 360 degrees of arc and 2π radians of arc; a triangle's angles add to 180°.

Everything else, from slope to the discriminant to surface area, you need to
know. The list, with links to where each is taught, is in
[Math formulas to know](learn:sat/general/math-reference).
