# Diagnostic Routing

Turning a practice-test result into a specific plan. Do this before choosing a
study plan — it determines what you actually work on.

---

## Step 1: Take a real diagnostic

Before studying anything, take one full, timed section of each test you're
considering. Real timing, one sitting, no phone, morning if possible.

If you have **official score reports from a previous sitting**, use those
instead — they're better data than any practice test, and they break down by
reporting category.

---

## Step 2: Classify every miss

Go through every wrong answer and label it with one of the four error types
from [Error log and review method](../07-error-log-and-review-method.md):

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Content** | Didn't know the rule, formula, or concept |
| **Process** | Knew it, applied it wrong |
| **Careless** | Would get it right on a redo |
| **Time** | Ran out, or rushed it |

**Redo each missed question untimed before labeling it.** If you get it now, it
was careless or time. If you still can't, it's content or process.

Then count.

---

## Step 3: Route on the dominant error type

This is the most important routing decision, and most students skip it. It
determines *what kind* of studying you should do.

| Dominant type | What it means | Your plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Content** (>40%) | Real knowledge gaps | Domain guides + targeted drilling. The most straightforward case. |
| **Process** (>40%) | You know things but misapply them | Untimed drilling, writing every step. Do **not** add speed work yet. |
| **Careless** (>35%) | Mechanics problem | **Stop studying content.** Two weeks of habit work. Highest ROI available. |
| **Time** (>30%) | Pacing failure | Checkpoint discipline, the 30-second rule, compressed practice. |
| **Even spread** | Broad weakness | Follow a full plan start to finish. |

### If careless errors dominate

This is common above a mid score, and it is good news — it's the fastest fix
available.

**Do this for two weeks instead of content study:**

1. Circle what the question asks, before solving. Every question.
2. Write every step. No mental arithmetic beyond one operation.
3. Rewrite the equation once before manipulating it.
4. Reread the stem before selecting.
5. Check sign, magnitude, and units on every math answer.

Drill at **80% speed** while building these. Speed comes back; the habits stay.

### If time errors dominate

Do **not** respond by trying to go faster on everything. That produces careless
errors and you've traded one problem for another.

Instead: [pacing work](../06-timing-and-pacing.md) — checkpoints, the
30-second rule, two-pass method, and deliberate triage practice.

---

## Step 4: Route on section and domain

Now find *where* the points are.

### Count misses by domain

For each section, count misses per catalog domain. Rank them.

**Your top two or three domains by miss count are your targets.** Ignore domains
with one or two misses — that's noise.

### Weight by domain size

A domain worth 15 questions deserves more attention than one worth 5, even at
the same accuracy.

| Test | Highest-value domains |
| --- | --- |
| SAT Math | Algebra (~14 q) and Advanced Math (~14 q) — 70% of the section |
| SAT RW | Standard English Conventions (~14 q) — finite rules, fastest gains |
| ACT English | Conventions (~19 q) and Production of Writing (~20 q) |
| ACT Math | Integrating Essential Skills (~9 q) — largest, and mostly procedural |
| ACT Reading | Key Ideas and Details (~17 q) — nearly half the section |
| ACT Science | Interpretation of Data (~17 q) — pure figure reading |

**If you're weak across the board**, start with the rows above. They're where
the questions are.

---

## Step 5: Check for the cheap wins

Before building a plan, check these. Each is a quick, high-yield fix that
students routinely miss.

| Check | If yes |
| --- | --- |
| Is SAT/ACT grammar accuracy below 85%? | Fix it first. Finite rules, 2-3 weeks, large gain. |
| Do you know the ACT formulas cold? | If not, handwrite [the reference](../act/math/07-formula-reference.md). No sheet is provided. |
| Are you fluent with Desmos? | If not, one week of [practice](../sat/math/06-desmos-playbook.md) is worth real points on SAT Math. |
| Do you orient before reading ACT Science figures? | If not, adopt the 10-second habit. Halves the error rate for most students. |
| Do you predict before looking at reading choices? | If not, start. It's the main defense against distractors. |
| Are your misses concentrated at section ends? | Pacing, not content. |
| Do you skip "EXCEPT" and "must be true" questions? | They're formulaic. Learn the two methods. |

---

## Step 6: Choose a plan

| Weeks until test | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| 3-5 | [4-week sprint](4-week-sprint.md) |
| 6-10 | [8-week standard](8-week-standard.md) |
| 11+ | [12-week foundation](12-week-foundation.md) |

If your target gain is large **and** you have under six weeks, be realistic:
prioritize careless errors, pacing, and grammar. Those move fastest. Deep
content rebuilding does not happen in a month.

## Worked routing example

> **Diagnostic:** ACT, Composite 24. English 26, Math 22, Reading 25, Science 23.
>
> **Miss counts:** English 12, Math 17, Reading 11, Science 14.
>
> **Error types across all sections:** Content 18, Process 12, Careless 19,
> Time 5.
>
> **Domain concentration:** Math misses cluster in Integrating Essential Skills
> (6) and Geometry (5). English misses cluster in Punctuation (7). Science
> misses spread across Interpretation of Data (8).

**Routing:**

1. **Careless is the largest bucket (19).** Two weeks of mechanics work runs in
   parallel with everything else — circle the target, write every step, reread
   the stem.
2. **Math is the weakest section and has the most misses.** Start with
   Integrating Essential Skills (largest domain, mostly procedural — overlaps
   with the careless fix) and Geometry (formula memorization, since the ACT
   provides none).
3. **English punctuation is 7 misses in a finite rule set.** Cheapest points
   available. Two weeks of the join table and the forbidden-comma list.
4. **Science is all Interpretation of Data.** Adopt the orientation habit; drill
   figure reading.
5. **Reading is the strongest relative section.** Maintenance only.
6. **Time errors are low (5).** Pacing isn't the problem. Don't spend time
   there.

**Resulting week-1 plan:** mechanics habits + English punctuation + ACT math
formula memorization. Three things, all fast-moving.

---

## Re-diagnosing

Repeat this process after each full practice test — roughly every two to three
weeks.

Two things to check:

1. **Did the targeted domains improve?** If not, your diagnosis was wrong.
   Re-examine: a "content" gap that persists after study is often actually a
   process problem underneath.
2. **What are the new top domains?** They'll change as you fix things. Follow
   them.

Do not keep drilling a domain you've already fixed because it was on your
original list. Follow the current data.
