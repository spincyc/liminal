# 4-Week Sprint

For a retake with a fixed near-term test date. Roughly **6-8 hours per week**.

Four weeks is enough to fix mechanics, pacing, and a finite rule set. It is
**not** enough to rebuild content knowledge from the ground up. Plan
accordingly: this schedule prioritizes the fastest-moving gains.

**Prerequisites:** run [Diagnostic routing](diagnostic-routing.md) first, and
read [Drill protocols](drill-protocols.md). The schedule below assumes both.

---

## What moves in four weeks

| Fast (do these) | Slow (don't start these now) |
| --- | --- |
| Careless-error habits | Rebuilding algebra from scratch |
| Grammar and punctuation rules | Learning trigonometry cold |
| Pacing and triage | Fundamentally faster reading |
| ACT formula memorization | New advanced-math topics |
| ACT Science orientation habit | Large vocabulary expansion |
| Desmos fluency | |
| Prediction habits on reading | |

If your diagnostic shows deep content gaps and you have four weeks, **accept
that** and maximize the left column. That's still a meaningful gain.

---

## Week 1 — Diagnose and fix mechanics

**Goal:** stop leaking points you've already earned.

| Day | Work | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Full timed section of each weak section. Classify every miss. | 2 h |
| 2 | Read [How questions are built](../04-how-questions-are-built.md) and [Universal answer patterns](../05-universal-answer-patterns.md). 20 mixed items applying them. | 1.5 h |
| 3 | Top weak domain: read the guide, 20 items untimed | 1.5 h |
| 4 | Grammar block — [SEC](../sat/reading-writing/04-standard-english-conventions.md) or [ACT Conventions](../act/english/03-conventions-of-standard-english.md). 30 items. | 1.5 h |
| 5 | Second weak domain: read the guide, 20 items untimed | 1.5 h |
| 6 | Missed-items review + 20 mixed items | 1 h |
| 7 | Rest, or light review of your rules list | — |

**Running all week:** the five careless-error habits.

1. Circle what the question asks, before solving
2. Write every step
3. Rewrite the equation before manipulating it
4. Reread the stem before selecting
5. Check sign, magnitude, and units

Drill at about 80% speed while building these. Speed returns; the habits stay.

---

## Week 2 — Content targets and rules

**Goal:** fix the two or three highest-value domains from your diagnostic.

| Day | Work | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Weak domain 1: 25 items, Easy → Medium | 1.5 h |
| 2 | Grammar block: 30 items timed at target pace | 1 h |
| 3 | Weak domain 2: 25 items, Easy → Medium | 1.5 h |
| 4 | Formula work — handwrite the relevant reference; 20 items applying it | 1.5 h |
| 5 | Weak domain 3, or more of domain 1 | 1.5 h |
| 6 | **Full practice test**, real conditions | 3 h |
| 7 | Same-day impressions (30 min). Rest. | 0.5 h |

**Also this week:** if you're taking the SAT, one week of 20-minute daily
[Desmos practice](../sat/math/06-desmos-playbook.md). If ACT Science,
adopt the [orientation habit](../act/science/00-overview.md) and narrate it
aloud on every figure.

---

## Week 3 — Pacing and consolidation

**Goal:** apply the skills at real speed.

| Day | Work | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Full test review, item by item. Extract three changes. | 2.5 h |
| 2 | Pacing drills: 2 sections at real timing with [checkpoints](../06-timing-and-pacing.md) | 1.5 h |
| 3 | Weakest remaining domain: 25 items timed | 1.5 h |
| 4 | Mixed-mode drill, 40 items, real timing | 1.5 h |
| 5 | Missed-items + flagged review | 1 h |
| 6 | Compressed practice: one section at **90% of real time** | 1 h |
| 7 | Rest | — |

**Pacing focus this week.** Write your checkpoints down at the start of every
timed block. Practice the **30-second rule** deliberately: when you don't know
how to start, mark, guess, move. It feels terrible and it's correct.

---

## Week 4 — Taper

**Goal:** arrive fluent and rested. No new material.

| Day | Work | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Final full practice test** (7-10 days out if possible) | 3 h |
| 2 | Review it. Extract at most two changes. | 2 h |
| 3 | Light mixed drilling, 30 items, mostly in strong skills | 1 h |
| 4 | Reread your rules list. 20 items in your weakest skill. | 1 h |
| 5 | Reread the [cheat sheets](../08-cheat-sheets.md) and your formula reference. 20 easy items. | 1 h |
| 6 | **Rest.** Logistics check: ID, calculator batteries, route, Bluebook setup. | 0.5 h |
| 7 | **Test day** | — |

### The last three days

- **No new material.** You can't learn a skill in three days and trying makes
  you anxious.
- **Light drilling only** — 20-30 items a day, mostly in skills you're good at.
  You want to walk in feeling fluent.
- **Reread your error-log rules list.** This is its entire payoff.
- **Sleep.** Two nights before matters more than the night before.
- **Do not take a full test the day before.**

---

## Compressed 2-week version

If you have only two weeks, run Week 1 and Week 4, and insert one content day
and one pacing day between them. Drop everything else.

Priority order when time is this short:

1. Careless-error habits
2. Grammar rules (finite, fast, high-volume)
3. Pacing and the 30-second rule
4. Formula memorization (ACT especially — no sheet provided)
5. One content domain, at most

---

## Expectations

Four weeks of serious work moves mechanics, pacing, and rule-based sections.
It does not rebuild mathematical foundations or make you a faster reader.

**Where the gain actually comes from, in a sprint:**

- Careless errors eliminated — often the largest single bucket
- Grammar accuracy raised from ~70% to ~90%
- Not running out of time
- Formula recall becoming automatic

Those are real points, and they are available to almost everyone in four weeks.
