# 12-Week Foundation Plan

For a large target jump, or shaky underlying content. Roughly **5-8 hours per
week** — lower weekly intensity than the sprint, sustained over three months.

Twelve weeks is enough to genuinely rebuild content knowledge, which the shorter
plans are not. The tradeoff is that it requires consistency over a long stretch,
and consistency is where most long plans fail.

**Prerequisites:** run [Diagnostic routing](diagnostic-routing.md) first, and
read [Drill protocols](drill-protocols.md).

---

## Structure

| Phase | Weeks | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I — Assess and repair** | 1-2 | Diagnose, fix mechanics, learn item architecture |
| **II — Rebuild** | 3-7 | Systematic content, including prerequisites |
| **III — Extend** | 8-9 | Hard difficulty, advanced topics |
| **IV — Speed** | 10-11 | Mixed practice, real and compressed timing |
| **V — Taper** | 12 | Consolidate, rest |

Five full practice tests: weeks 1, 4, 7, 10, and 11.

---

## Phase I — Assess and repair (weeks 1-2)

Identical in intent to the 8-week plan's Phase I, but with room to be thorough.

### Week 1

| Day | Work | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Full diagnostic test**, real conditions | 3 h |
| 2 | Classify every miss. Complete [diagnostic routing](diagnostic-routing.md). | 2 h |
| 3 | Read [How questions are built](../04-how-questions-are-built.md). 20 mixed items applying it. | 1.5 h |
| 4 | Read [Universal answer patterns](../05-universal-answer-patterns.md). 20 items. | 1.5 h |
| 5 | Read section overviews. Set up your error log. | 1 h |
| 6 | 20 mixed items with full review | 1 h |
| 7 | Rest | — |

### Week 2 — mechanics

The five careless-error habits, at 80% speed all week.

| Day | Work | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Weak domain 1: read the guide, 15 items untimed | 1.5 h |
| 2 | Grammar: read the conventions guide, 25 items | 1.5 h |
| 3 | Weak domain 1: 20 items untimed, every step written | 1.5 h |
| 4 | Formula reference — **handwrite it once** | 1 h |
| 5 | Grammar: 30 items | 1.5 h |
| 6 | Missed-items + weekly audit | 1 h |
| 7 | Rest | — |

## Phase II — Rebuild (weeks 3-7)

The distinguishing phase of this plan: enough time to fix **prerequisites**, not
just tested skills.

### Prerequisite repair

If your diagnostic showed deep content gaps, the underlying problem is often a
level below what's tested. Common examples:

| Struggling with | Likely prerequisite gap |
| --- | --- |
| Systems of equations | Solving single linear equations reliably |
| Quadratics | Factoring, and the distributive property with negatives |
| Exponential models | Percent as a multiplier |
| Rational expressions | Fraction arithmetic |
| Trigonometry | Similar triangles and ratios |
| Data questions | Reading axes and units |
| Inference questions | Distinguishing stated from implied |
| Any word problem | Translating English to algebra |

**Spend the first week of Phase II on prerequisites** if your diagnostic
warrants it. Drill Easy items until you're at 90%+ before moving up. Building
Advanced Math on shaky algebra doesn't work, and twelve weeks is enough time to
do it properly.

### The per-domain cycle

Each domain gets roughly four sessions in this plan:

| Session | Work |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Read the guide. 15 items **Easy, untimed**. |
| 2 | 20 items **Medium, untimed**. Full review. |
| 3 | 20 items **Medium, timed**. Full review. |
| 4 | 15 items **Hard, untimed**. Full review. |

### Weeks 3-7 rhythm

Each week:

| Days | Work |
| --- | --- |
| 1-4 | Domain work — roughly one domain per week, four sessions |
| 5 | Grammar or Science maintenance, 25-30 items |
| 6 | Missed-items + weekly audit, or a full test on weeks 4 and 7 |
| 7 | Rest |

**Full practice tests in weeks 4 and 7**, with full review the following day.

**Re-route after each test.** Your target list will change as you fix things.
Follow the current data, not your original plan.

### Suggested domain order

Work weakest-first, but weight by domain size. Rough default ordering if
everything is weak:

**SAT:** Standard English Conventions → Algebra → Advanced Math →
Information and Ideas → Craft and Structure → Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
→ Expression of Ideas → Geometry and Trigonometry

**ACT:** Conventions of Standard English → Integrating Essential Skills →
Key Ideas and Details → Interpretation of Data → Algebra → Production of
Writing → Functions → Geometry → the rest

Conventions and the largest math domain come first in both cases: finite rules
and the biggest point pools.

## Phase III — Extend (weeks 8-9)

Content is functional. Now push into the hard third.

### Week 8

| Day | Work | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Hard-difficulty drill**, domain A, 15 items untimed | 1.5 h |
| 2 | Hard drill, domain B, 15 items untimed | 1.5 h |
| 3 | Advanced topics you've been avoiding — logs, trig graphs, counting, complex numbers | 1.5 h |
| 4 | Hard drill, domain C, 15 items | 1.5 h |
| 5 | Cross-domain Hard mix, 20 items | 1.5 h |
| 6 | Missed-items + audit | 1 h |
| 7 | Rest | — |

### Week 9

Same shape, different domains. Also:

- **Reread [How questions are built](../04-how-questions-are-built.md).** It
  reads differently once you've done 500 items, and hard questions are where
  distractor architecture matters most.
- Work the question types you've been skipping: "must be true," EXCEPT, two-part
  answers, paired passages, Conflicting Viewpoints.

**Hard items should be untimed at this stage.** The point is extending your
ceiling, not speed. Speed comes in Phase IV.

## Phase IV — Speed (weeks 10-11)

### Week 10

| Day | Work | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pacing drills: 2 sections at real timing with [checkpoints](../06-timing-and-pacing.md) | 1.5 h |
| 2 | **Mixed-mode**, 40 items, real timing | 1.5 h |
| 3 | Compressed: one section at **90% of real time** | 1 h |
| 4 | Mixed-mode, 40 items | 1.5 h |
| 5 | Missed-items + flagged review | 1 h |
| 6 | **Full practice test** | 3 h |
| 7 | Same-day impressions. Rest. | 0.5 h |

### Week 11

| Day | Work | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Test review. Extract three changes. | 2.5 h |
| 2 | Targeted work on what the test exposed | 1.5 h |
| 3 | Mixed-mode, 40 items, real timing | 1.5 h |
| 4 | Compressed, one section at 90% | 1 h |
| 5 | Missed-items + reread your rules list | 1 h |
| 6 | **Final full practice test** (7-10 days before the real one) | 3 h |
| 7 | Rest | — |

---

## Phase V — Taper (week 12)

| Day | Work | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Final test review. At most two changes. | 2 h |
| 2 | Light mixed drilling, 30 items, mostly strong skills | 1 h |
| 3 | Reread your rules list. 20 items in your weakest skill. | 1 h |
| 4 | Reread the [cheat sheets](../08-cheat-sheets.md) and your formula reference. 20 easy items. | 1 h |
| 5 | 20 warm-up items. **Logistics check.** | 0.5 h |
| 6 | **Rest.** Lay everything out. Sleep early. | — |
| 7 | **Test day** | — |

## Sustaining a 12-week plan

Long plans fail for predictable reasons. Guard against them:

**Week 5-7 is the danger zone.** Novelty is gone, the test is still distant,
motivation drops. Get through it by keeping sessions short and consistent rather
than long and sporadic. Four 60-minute sessions beat two 3-hour ones.

**Schedule it.** Fixed days and times. "When I have time" produces nothing over
twelve weeks.

**Track something visible.** Per-skill accuracy in the app's Progress view, or a
simple count of items completed. Visible progress sustains effort when the score
isn't moving yet.

**Take a real break** — one full week off around week 6 or 7 if you need it.
Burning out at week 8 costs more than a lost week.

**Don't over-test.** Five full tests across twelve weeks is right. More burns
material and energy for diminishing returns.

**Expect a plateau.** Scores typically move in steps, not smoothly. Two weeks of
flat practice-test scores while your per-skill accuracy improves is normal and
is not a reason to change the plan.

---

## Adapting

**If you're ahead of schedule:** extend Phase III. Hard-difficulty work has the
highest ceiling and most students under-invest in it.

**If you're behind:** cut Phase III to one week and preserve Phase IV. Pacing
and mixed practice matter more than the hardest content.

**If you're testing both SAT and ACT:** run this plan for one test, then a
[4-week sprint](4-week-sprint.md) for the other, focused on format differences.
Content transfers; format doesn't.

**If a domain won't improve after four sessions:** the diagnosis is wrong. A
persistent content gap is usually a prerequisite gap a level below. Drop down a
level and rebuild.
