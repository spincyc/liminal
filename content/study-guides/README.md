# Liminal Study Guides

A complete, self-contained study library for the digital SAT and the enhanced
ACT. This is the *knowledge* half of Liminal: the question bank in
`content/banks/` gives you 4,025 items to practice on, and these guides tell you
what to actually know, what patterns to look for, and how to work.

Everything here is original material. Nothing reproduces official test
questions.

---

## Start here

If you have never used this library, read these three files in order. They take
about forty minutes total and they change how everything else lands.

1. [How to use this guide](00-how-to-use-this-guide.md) — the study method, and
   why passive reading does almost nothing for your score.
2. [Test formats and scoring](01-test-formats-and-scoring.md) — what each test
   actually is in 2026-27, including the digital SAT's adaptive structure and
   the enhanced ACT's optional Science section.
3. [How questions are built](04-how-questions-are-built.md) — the single
   highest-leverage file in this library. Standardized test items are
   manufactured to a spec. Once you can see the spec, wrong answers start
   announcing themselves.

Then pick a study plan from [`plans/`](plans/) and start drilling.

## How these guides connect to the app

Every domain guide names the exact catalog domain and skills it covers, so you
can read a guide and then filter the app to precisely that material.

- Guides map to `content/catalog.json` domains and skills.
- The [Answer Signs](../guides/answer-signs.js) view in the app is the
  condensed, in-browser version of
  [05 — Universal answer patterns](05-universal-answer-patterns.md) and the
  per-section pattern sections. The guides here are longer and include worked
  reasoning; Answer Signs is the quick-reference card.
- Practice sessions filter by section, domain, skill, and difficulty. Guides are
  organized the same way on purpose.

## An honest word about "patterns"

This library leans hard on patterns: how items are built, how distractors are
manufactured, which answer shapes survive scrutiny. That is genuinely useful
knowledge and it is completely legitimate — you are learning how a
well-documented assessment is designed, not exploiting it.

But patterns are a *second* line of attack, not a first. A student who
pattern-matches without reading gets destroyed by the hard third of either test,
because item writers explicitly design hard questions to punish surface
heuristics. The order is always:

1. Understand the question.
2. Solve it or reason it out from the text.
3. Use patterns to check your answer, break a genuine tie, or guess when the
   clock is gone.

Every heuristic in this library is stated with the conditions under which it
fails. Read those parts.
