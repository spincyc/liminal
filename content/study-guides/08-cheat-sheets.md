# Cheat Sheets

Condensed one-page references. These are the pages to reread **the day before**
your test — not the full guides.

Everything here is covered in depth elsewhere in this library and, for the
SAT, in the app's [Learn pages](https://spincyc.github.io/liminal/learn.html).
This file exists because on the night before a test you want density, not
explanation.

## Universal — both tests

### The order of operations for every question

1. **Understand** what's asked. Circle it.
2. **Solve or locate** from the math or the text.
3. **Check** using patterns.
4. **Guess** among choices you have not ruled out when time is gone.

### Elimination cascade

1. Check the actual task, including **NOT** or **EXCEPT**.
2. Eliminate choices that contradict the text, data or stated conditions.
3. Check the required scope, units and quantity.
4. If choices still tie and time is gone, guess among the survivors.

Word length, hedging, opposite pairs and clean numbers do not establish the
answer. Elimination helps only when the removed choice is actually wrong.

### Five rules that are close to absolute

- **Check the whole choice against the task.** A false-claim or EXCEPT question
  reverses what you are looking for.
- **Answer the question that was asked.** Not the intermediate value.
- **Never leave anything blank.** No wrong-answer penalty on either test.
- **Your answer being listed confirms nothing.** Distractors are built from
  standard errors.
- **Identify supporting evidence.** A reading inference may combine several
  details rather than match one line.

### The 30-second rule

No idea how to *start* after 30 seconds? Mark it, guess your default letter,
move. Two minutes on one hard question costs three easy ones.

## Digital SAT

### Timing

| Module | Q | Time | Checkpoints |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RW (×2) | 27 | 32 min | Q7@8, Q14@16, Q21@24, done@30 |
| Math (×2) | 22 | 35 min | Q6@6, Q12@15, Q18@25, done@33 |

Module 1 determines your Module 2 routing. **Don't warm up through it.** A
hard-feeling Module 2 is usually good news.

### RW question order

Craft and Structure → Information and Ideas → Standard English Conventions →
Expression of Ideas

Within each domain, questions group by skill, then difficulty; Conventions
runs by difficulty across the domain. Later groups can contain easier
questions. You may navigate freely within a module.

### Grid-in rules

- 5 characters (6 with a minus sign)
- No commas, `$`, `%`, or units
- **No mixed numbers** — enter 3½ as `7/2` or `3.5`
- Long decimals to the fourth digit: `.6666` or `.6667`, not `.67`
- Negatives allowed. One answer if several are valid.

### Reading method

1. Read the **stem** first
2. Read the whole (short) passage — find the **pivot word**
3. **Predict** in your own words
4. Eliminate against the prediction
5. Point to the supporting text

### Punctuation join table

| Before | After | Legal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IND | IND | `.` `;` `, + FANBOYS` `:` `—` |
| IND | DEP | `,` `:` `—` or nothing |
| DEP | IND | `,` |
| Phrase | Phrase | usually nothing |

**Free kills:** semicolon and period doing the same job → both wrong (fails
if the two choices differ in anything else). One-sided interrupter punctuation
→ wrong (fails when the interrupter starts or ends the sentence). Comma
between subject and verb → wrong. Comma splice → wrong. Colon without a full
sentence before it → wrong.

**`However` is not a conjunction.** `X, however, Y` joining two clauses is a
splice.

### Desmos moves

- Graph both sides → click the intersection
- Type systems as given (no need to solve for `y`)
- Sliders for "for what value of `k`"
- Graph the original and each choice for equivalence
- `mean()`, `median()`, `stdev()` on lists

### Top SAT traps

Solving for the wrong variable · forgetting `±` · not flipping an inequality ·
percent change denominator · `f(x+2)` shifted right · composition order ·
diameter for radius · half-right reading choices · word-match traps

## Enhanced ACT

### Timing

| Section | Q | Time | Per Q | Checkpoints |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| English | 50 | 35 | 42 s | 7 min per passage |
| Math | 45 | 50 | 67 s | Q15@12, Q28@25, Q38@38 |
| Reading | 36 | 40 | 67 s | 10 min per passage, hard stop |
| Science | 40 | 40 | 60 s | 5-6 min per passage |

Composite = **English + Math + Reading only**. Science is separate.
Math has **4 choices**. **No formula sheet.**

### English

**Vertical scan first** — what varies across the choices tells you the rule.

Same join table and free kills as the SAT (above).

Three ACT principles (not SAT rules): **shorter is better** · **clearer is
better** · **relevance rules**.

- **DELETE/OMIT** is a real candidate whenever the words add nothing
- **NO CHANGE** is right about 25% of the time — don't avoid it
- On any **stated-goal** question, the goal is the entire rubric
- Transitions: **cover the choices**, name the relationship, then look

Target 22 seconds on convention questions. That funds the rhetorical ones.

### Reading

10 minutes per passage. **Passage order is yours** — do your worst type last.

Per passage: ~3 min reading, ~7 min questions.

- **Go back to the text.** Especially when you remember.
- **Paired passages:** read A → answer A → read B → answer B → then comparison.
  One sentence per author before comparing.
- **Author ≠ the view being described.** Look for `however`, `unfortunately`,
  `convincingly`.
- Attitude: identify its direction and strength from the text. Strong wording
  and `indifferent` can be correct when supported.

### Science

**It combines data reading, scientific reasoning and introductory science knowledge.**

**Ten-second orientation, every figure:** title · x-axis · y-axis · units ·
**second y-axis?** · legend · linear or log · does the axis start at zero.

- Go **straight to the questions** on Data Representation and Research
  Summaries
- **Conflicting Viewpoints must be read** — budget 7 min, one sentence per
  viewpoint, and name the viewpoint before reading choices
- **Direction first** — up or down eliminates half the choices
- **Trends beat single points**
- Independent = x-axis / what changed. Dependent = y-axis / what was measured.
- "Held constant in order to..." → **to isolate the variable being tested**
- Match a follow-up trial to its purpose: isolate a factor, test a new
  condition, or repeat a condition to check reliability
- Conclusions can't exceed the range tested

### Top ACT traps

Intermediate value · area/volume unit conversion · permutation vs. combination ·
with/without replacement · averaged the averages · wrong viewpoint on
Conflicting Viewpoints · second y-axis · dangling modifier · `however` as a
conjunction · avoided NO CHANGE · didn't flip the inequality

## Test-morning checklist

- [ ] Admission ticket
- [ ] Photo ID (name matches registration exactly)
- [ ] Pencils (No. 2)
- [ ] Approved calculator + **fresh batteries**
- [ ] Watch — no alarm, no smart features
- [ ] Snack and water for the break
- [ ] Layers
- [ ] **SAT only:** charged device, Bluebook installed and set up, charger
- [ ] Phone **fully off**, not vibrate
- [ ] Arrive by the time on your admission ticket; doors close at the stated
      time and late arrivals are turned away

### During the test

- Write your **checkpoints** on scratch paper in the first ten seconds
- Check the clock **at checkpoints only**
- Prioritize questions you can answer reliably. **SAT scaled scoring is not
  a fixed point value per question.** On ACT, scored multiple-choice items
  contribute equally to the raw correct count before score conversion.
- **Don't carry a bad section.** Sections score separately.
- Break: eat, drink, walk. Don't discuss the test.
- Five-minute warning: stop starting new hard questions. Sweep for blanks.
