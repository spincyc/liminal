# Grammar Reference

Every grammar rule tested on the SAT and ACT, with examples. This is a reference
to look things up in, not a document to read start to finish.

Applies to **both tests**. ACT English tests the same rule set with somewhat
more emphasis on punctuation volume and rhetorical questions; see
[ACT Conventions of Standard English](../../act/english/03-conventions-of-standard-english.md)
for the ACT-specific framing.

## 1. Sentence structure

### Independent and dependent clauses

**Independent clause** — subject + verb, expresses a complete thought, can stand
alone.
> *The experiment failed.*

**Dependent (subordinate) clause** — has a subject and verb but begins with a
subordinating word, cannot stand alone.
> *Because the experiment failed...*

**Phrase** — no subject-verb pair.
> *After the failure of the experiment...*

### Subordinating conjunctions

Words that make a clause dependent:

> after · although · as · as if · because · before · even though · if · once ·
> since · so that · than · that · though · unless · until · when · whenever ·
> where · whereas · wherever · whether · while

### Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS)

> **F**or · **A**nd · **N**or · **B**ut · **O**r · **Y**et · **S**o

Only these seven join two independent clauses with a comma.

### Conjunctive adverbs

> however · therefore · moreover · nevertheless · consequently · furthermore ·
> thus · otherwise · instead · indeed · accordingly · similarly · likewise ·
> meanwhile · subsequently

**These are not conjunctions.** They cannot join independent clauses with a
comma.

> ✗ *The data were incomplete, however the team proceeded.*
> ✓ *The data were incomplete; however, the team proceeded.*
> ✓ *The data were incomplete. However, the team proceeded.*
> ✓ *The data were incomplete, but the team proceeded.*

### Run-ons and comma splices

**Run-on (fused sentence):** two independent clauses with no punctuation.
> ✗ *It rained we stayed inside.*

**Comma splice:** two independent clauses joined by a comma alone.
> ✗ *It rained, we stayed inside.*

**Five legal fixes:**

| Fix | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Period | *It rained. We stayed inside.* |
| Semicolon | *It rained; we stayed inside.* |
| Comma + FANBOYS | *It rained, so we stayed inside.* |
| Subordinate one clause | *Because it rained, we stayed inside.* |
| Colon (if second explains first) | *The reason was simple: it rained.* |

### Fragments

A fragment lacks a subject, lacks a finite verb, or is a dependent clause
standing alone.

| Fragment type | Example | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No subject | *Ran the entire distance.* | *She ran the entire distance.* |
| No finite verb | *The team running the trial.* | *The team **was** running the trial.* |
| Dependent alone | *Although the results were promising.* | Attach to an independent clause |
| Participial phrase alone | *Having reviewed the data.* | Attach to an independent clause |

**Note:** an *-ing* word alone is not a finite verb. *The scientist working late*
is a fragment; *The scientist **was** working late* is a sentence.

## 2. Punctuation

### The join table

| Before | After | Legal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Independent | Independent | Period · Semicolon · Comma + FANBOYS · Colon (if explanatory) · Dash |
| Independent | Dependent/phrase | Comma · Colon · Dash · Nothing |
| Dependent | Independent | Comma |
| Phrase | Phrase | Usually nothing |

### Comma — required uses

| Use | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Before FANBOYS joining independents | *She studied**,** and he rested.* |
| After introductory word/phrase/clause | *In 1922**,** the tomb was opened.* |
| Both sides of non-essential material | *The report**,** issued Monday**,** was long.* |
| Between list items | *red**,** blue**,** and green* |
| Between coordinate adjectives | *a long**,** tedious process* |
| Before a tag or contrast | *It was cheap**,** not free.* |

**Coordinate adjectives test:** if you can insert *and* between them or reverse
them and it still reads correctly, use a comma. *A long and tedious process* ✓ →
comma. *A bright red car* — you wouldn't say *bright and red car* → no comma.

### Comma — forbidden uses

| Never | Wrong |
| --- | --- |
| Between subject and verb | *The results of the trial**,** were clear.* |
| Between verb and object | *She found**,** the answer.* |
| Between two verbs sharing a subject | *He read the study**,** and cited it.* |
| Around essential clauses | *The car**,** that I bought**,** is red.* |
| Before a closing dependent clause | *He left**,** because it rained.* |
| After a coordinating conjunction | *but**,** the results differed* |
| Between an adjective and its noun | *a difficult**,** problem* |

### Essential vs. non-essential

| | Punctuation | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Essential** (restrictive) | None | Removing it changes which thing you mean |
| **Non-essential** (non-restrictive) | Commas/dashes/parens both sides | Removing it leaves the meaning intact |

> *Students **who cheated** were expelled.* (only the cheaters — essential)
> *Students**, who cheated,** were expelled.* (all students, and they cheated —
> non-essential)

**That vs. which:**
- *That* introduces essential clauses — no comma.
- *Which* introduces non-essential clauses — comma.

> *The book **that** I ordered arrived.*
> *The book**, which** I ordered last week, arrived.*

### Semicolon

Two uses only:

1. **Join two independent clauses** without a conjunction.
   > *The trial ended early; the funding had lapsed.*
2. **Separate list items that themselves contain commas.**
   > *The team visited Lima, Peru; Quito, Ecuador; and Bogotá, Colombia.*

**A semicolon is functionally a period.** If a period doesn't work there, a
semicolon doesn't either.

### Colon

**Requires a complete sentence before it.** What follows may be a list, a phrase,
a single word, or a clause.

> ✓ *The team needed three things: funding, equipment, and time.*
> ✓ *The conclusion was inescapable: the model was wrong.*
> ✗ *The team needed: funding, equipment, and time.*
> ✗ *The three components are: soil, water, and light.*

That last pattern — a colon after *are*, *include*, *such as*, or *for example*
— is a common wrong answer.

### Dash

**Single dash:** introduces an explanation or a break, like a colon or comma.
> *The result was unexpected — no one had predicted it.*

**Paired dashes:** set off a non-essential interruption, like paired commas.
> *The finding — first reported in 1998 — has since been replicated.*

**Punctuation must match on both sides.** A dash cannot pair with a comma.

> ✗ *The finding — first reported in 1998, has since been replicated.*

Dashes are the strongest interrupter, commas the mildest, parentheses the most
aside-like. All three are grammatically interchangeable for non-essential
material; the SAT and ACT test only that you pair them correctly.

### Apostrophe

| Purpose | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular possessive | `'s` | *the dog's leash* |
| Plural possessive (ends in s) | `'` | *the dogs' leashes* |
| Plural possessive (irregular) | `'s` | *the children's books* |
| Singular noun ending in s | `'s` | *the class's schedule* |
| Contraction | replaces missing letters | *don't, it's, they're* |
| **Plural nouns** | **no apostrophe** | *three dogs* |

**Never use an apostrophe to make a noun plural.** This is the most common
apostrophe error and it appears on both tests.

**Possessive pronouns take no apostrophe:** *its, hers, his, ours, yours,
theirs, whose.*

## 3. Verbs

### Subject-verb agreement

The verb agrees with the **subject**, not with the nearest noun.

**Method:** find the subject, cross out everything between it and the verb.

| Hiding construction | Example | Real subject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepositional phrase | *The **box** of nails **is** heavy.* | box |
| Relative clause | *The **students** who took the test **were** tired.* | students |
| Appositive | ***Curie**, the physicist, **was** awarded...* | Curie |
| Inversion | *Among the results **were** three anomalies.* | three anomalies |
| Expletive | *There **are** several reasons.* | several reasons |

**Special rules:**

| Rule | Example |
| --- | --- |
| *along with, as well as, in addition to, together with, including* don't change number | *The **teacher**, along with the students, **was** late.* |
| *either/or*, *neither/nor* → agrees with **nearer** subject | *Neither the students nor the **teacher is** here.* |
| Compound subject with *and* → plural | *The **cat and dog are** asleep.* |
| Collective nouns → singular | *The **committee has** decided.* |
| Indefinite pronouns → singular | *each, every, either, neither, one, none, everyone, anybody, nobody, somebody* |
| *Some, most, all, none, any* → depend on the object | *Most of the **water is** gone. Most of the **books are** gone.* |
| Gerund subject → singular | ***Running** marathons **is** hard.* |
| Titles and amounts → singular | *Ten dollars **is** enough.* |

### Tense

| Tense | Form | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple present | *walks* | Habitual, general truth |
| Simple past | *walked* | Completed past action |
| Simple future | *will walk* | Future action |
| Present perfect | *has walked* | Started in past, continues, or unspecified time |
| Past perfect | *had walked* | Completed **before** another past action |
| Future perfect | *will have walked* | Completed before a future point |
| Present progressive | *is walking* | Ongoing now |
| Past progressive | *was walking* | Ongoing in the past |

**Past perfect is the tested one.** Use it for the earlier of two past events.

> *By the time the results arrived, the team **had already published**.*

**Consistency:** don't shift tense without a signal. Narrative passages stay in
one tense; time markers (*in 1890*, *today*, *since then*) license shifts.

### Irregular verb forms

The past participle (used with *has/have/had*) differs from the simple past.

| Base | Past | Participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| begin | began | begun |
| break | broke | broken |
| choose | chose | chosen |
| drink | drank | drunk |
| drive | drove | driven |
| fall | fell | fallen |
| go | went | gone |
| know | knew | known |
| lie (recline) | lay | lain |
| lay (place) | laid | laid |
| ride | rode | ridden |
| rise | rose | risen |
| see | saw | seen |
| shrink | shrank | shrunk |
| sink | sank | sunk |
| speak | spoke | spoken |
| swim | swam | swum |
| take | took | taken |
| write | wrote | written |

> ✗ *He had went.* ✓ *He had gone.*
> ✗ *She has took it.* ✓ *She has taken it.*

### Mood and conditionals

**Subjunctive** for hypotheticals contrary to fact:
> *If I **were** taller...* (not *was*)

**Subjunctive** after verbs of demand or recommendation:
> *The board recommended that he **resign**.* (not *resigns*)

**Conditional consistency:**
> *If the temperature **had risen**, the reaction **would have occurred**.*
> ✗ *If the temperature **would have** risen...*

## 4. Pronouns

### Case

| Subject | Object | Possessive | Reflexive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | me | my/mine | myself |
| you | you | your/yours | yourself |
| he/she/it | him/her/it | his/her/its | himself/herself/itself |
| we | us | our/ours | ourselves |
| they | them | their/theirs | themselves |
| who | whom | whose | — |

**Compound test:** remove the other person.
> *Between you and **me**.* (You wouldn't say *between I*.)
> *She gave it to Sarah and **me**.*

**Who vs. whom:** substitute *he/him*.
> *The scientist **who** discovered it* → *he discovered it* → *who*
> *The scientist **whom** we consulted* → *we consulted him* → *whom*

**Never use a reflexive as a subject or plain object.**
> ✗ *Myself and John attended.* ✓ *John and I attended.*
> ✗ *Please contact myself.* ✓ *Please contact me.*

### Agreement

A pronoun matches its antecedent in **number**.

> ✗ *Each student must bring **their** book.* (on these tests)
> ✓ *Each student must bring **his or her** book.*
> ✓ *Students must bring **their** books.*

Organizations are **it**:
> ✓ *The company announced **its** results.*

### Ambiguity

If a pronoun could refer to more than one noun, it's an error.

> ✗ *When Maria met Elena, **she** was nervous.*
> ✓ *When Maria met Elena, **Maria** was nervous.*

### Commonly confused

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **its** possessive | **it's** it is/has |
| **their** possessive | **they're** they are | **there** location |
| **whose** possessive | **who's** who is/has |
| **your** possessive | **you're** you are |

## 5. Modifiers

### Dangling modifiers

An opening phrase must modify the subject that follows it.

> ✗ *Walking through the forest, the birds were loud.*
> ✓ *Walking through the forest, **we** heard the birds.*
>
> ✗ *To qualify for the grant, the application must be complete.*
> ✓ *To qualify for the grant, **you** must complete the application.*

**Check:** read the opening phrase, then look at the very next noun. Did that
noun do the action?

### Misplaced modifiers

Put the modifier next to what it modifies.

> ✗ *She **almost** drove her kids to school every day.* (she almost drove?)
> ✓ *She drove her kids to school **almost** every day.*

**Limiting words** — *only, almost, just, nearly, even, hardly, merely* — change
meaning based on placement. Put them immediately before what they limit.

> *Only she said that.* / *She only said that.* / *She said only that.*

### Adjective vs. adverb

Adjectives modify nouns; adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs.

> ✗ *She ran **quick**.* ✓ *She ran **quickly**.*
> ✗ *He felt **badly** about it.* ✓ *He felt **bad** about it.* (linking verb)

**Linking verbs** (*be, seem, appear, become, feel, look, sound, taste, smell*)
take adjectives, not adverbs, when describing the subject.

> *The soup tastes **good**.* (not *well*)
> *She looks **tired**.* (not *tiredly*)

## 6. Parallelism and comparison

### Parallelism

Items in a series, comparison, or correlative pair share grammatical form.

> ✗ *reading, writing, and **to speak***
> ✓ *reading, writing, and **speaking***
>
> ✗ *The job requires **patience**, **skill**, and **being organized**.*
> ✓ *The job requires **patience**, **skill**, and **organization**.*

**Correlative pairs** — the structures after each half must match:

| Pair | Example |
| --- | --- |
| not only ... but also | *not only **cheap** but also **fast*** |
| either ... or | *either **by train** or **by bus*** |
| neither ... nor | *neither **wrote** nor **called*** |
| both ... and | *both **the design** and **the execution*** |
| not ... but | *not **a failure** but **a delay*** |

### Comparison

**Compare like things.**

> ✗ *The population of Tokyo is larger than **Paris**.*
> ✓ *The population of Tokyo is larger than **that of Paris**.*
> ✓ *Tokyo's population is larger than **Paris's**.*

**Comparative vs. superlative:**
- Two things → comparative (*-er*, *more*): *the **taller** of the two*
- Three or more → superlative (*-est*, *most*): *the **tallest** in the class*

**Never double:** ✗ *more taller*, ✗ *most tallest*

**Countable vs. uncountable:**

| Countable | Uncountable |
| --- | --- |
| fewer | less |
| number | amount |
| many | much |

> *fewer **cars**, less **traffic*** · *the number of **errors**, the amount of
> **time***

**Between vs. among:** *between* two, *among* three or more.

## 8. Concision and redundancy

Both tests treat wordiness as an **error**, not a style preference. When two
choices are grammatically correct and mean the same thing, the shorter one is
credited.

### Redundant pairs

Each of these says one thing twice:

> annual yearly · past history · future plans · advance warning · basic
> fundamentals · combine together · completely finish · each and every ·
> end result · exact same · final outcome · free gift · joint collaboration ·
> new innovation · personal opinion · repeat again · return back · sudden
> impulse · sufficient enough · true fact · unexpected surprise ·
> the reason is because · in my opinion, I think

### Wordy phrases

| Wordy | Concise |
| --- | --- |
| due to the fact that | because |
| in spite of the fact that | although |
| at this point in time | now |
| in the event that | if |
| for the purpose of | to |
| has the ability to | can |
| in order to | to |
| a large number of | many |
| is able to | can |
| make a decision | decide |
| with regard to | about |
| prior to | before |
| subsequent to | after |
| in the near future | soon |

### The "DELETE" option

When *"DELETE the underlined portion"* or *"OMIT"* appears as a choice, it is
credited more often than 25% of the time — redundancy and irrelevance are heavily
tested.

**Take it when** the material repeats something already stated, is irrelevant to
the paragraph, or merely decorates.

**Don't take it when** the material provides necessary information, a required
transition, or a grammatically required element.

## Quick diagnostic checklist

When you can't identify what's being tested, run down this list:

1. Is each option a complete sentence? (fragments, run-ons)
2. Does the subject match the verb? (find the subject, cross out the middle)
3. Is the tense consistent with the passage?
4. Does every pronoun have a clear, matching antecedent?
5. Do the opening phrase and the following subject agree logically?
6. Are list items in the same form?
7. Are compared things the same kind of thing?
8. Is any punctuation separating a subject from its verb?
9. Is any interrupter punctuated on only one side?
10. Are two choices doing the identical job? (both wrong)
11. Is the shortest correct option available?
