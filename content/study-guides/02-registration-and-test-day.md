# Registration and Test Day

Logistics lose more points than people admit. A missed deadline costs you an
entire testing cycle; a forgotten calculator costs you a section.

> **Verify before you rely on this.** Dates, fees, and policies change. This
> file reflects the published 2026-27 schedule as of August 2026. Always confirm
> against [satsuite.collegeboard.org](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/registration/dates-deadlines)
> and [act.org](https://www.act.org/content/act/en/products-and-services/the-act/registration/test-dates.html).

## Working backward from an application deadline

This is the calculation that actually determines your test date. Do it first.

```
Application deadline
  − 1 week safety margin
  − score release lag (SAT ~2 weeks, ACT ~2-4 weeks)
  = latest usable test date
```

**Worked example, Nov 1 deadline:**

- Latest usable SAT: subtract 3 weeks → **Oct 3 is safe, Nov 7 is not.**
- Latest usable ACT: subtract 5 weeks → **Sept 19 is safe, Oct 17 is a gamble.**

The asymmetry catches people. The ACT's slower score release means its
effective deadline is roughly two weeks earlier than the SAT's for the same
application date.

**Caveats that can rescue a late date:**

- If a college accepts **self-reported scores**, you only need to *see* your
  score before the deadline, not have the official report delivered. That can
  buy you two weeks.
- Some colleges accept scores from a test date shortly after the deadline if you
  register in advance and notify them. Ask admissions directly — they answer
  this question constantly and are not annoyed by it.

## Fee waivers

Both tests offer waivers, and they are worth real money.

**You likely qualify if any of these apply:**

- Enrolled in or eligible for the National School Lunch Program (free/reduced
  lunch)
- Family income at or below USDA Food and Nutrition Service income eligibility
  levels
- Enrolled in a federal, state, or local program that aids students from
  low-income families (e.g., Upward Bound)
- Housing is federally subsidized
- In foster care, a ward of the state, or recently aged out
- Homeless or unaccompanied youth
- An orphan

**How to get one:** ask your school counselor. Waivers are issued through the
school, not by applying online yourself. If you are homeschooled or out of
school, contact an agency or community organization — both testing
organizations document alternate routes.

**What a waiver covers** (approximately — verify current terms):

- The test registration fee for multiple test dates
- Score reports to colleges
- Waived late fees in some circumstances
- Related college application fee waivers, which are often worth more than the
  test waivers themselves

Do not pay before checking. Students who qualify frequently don't ask.

## Testing accommodations

If you need extended time, extra breaks, a separate room, a reader, assistive
technology, or any other accommodation:

- **Start immediately.** Approval commonly takes around **seven weeks**, and
  requests can be sent back for more documentation, which restarts the clock.
- Requests go through your school's accommodations coordinator (SSD coordinator
  for College Board, similar role for ACT).
- Approval is **per testing organization**. An SAT approval does not carry over
  to the ACT, or vice versa. You must request both.
- An IEP or 504 plan helps but does not guarantee approval; the organizations
  apply their own standards.
- English learner supports are a separate category with their own process.

If you are within seven weeks of your target date and don't yet have approval,
plan for the following test date instead.

## Choosing a test center

- Register **early** for the center you want. Popular centers fill, and late
  registrants get assigned whatever is left, which can mean a long drive.
- Search by **city name, not metro area**. Surrounding suburbs are listed
  separately, so search several nearby towns to see everything within range.
- If you can, **visit or look up the location beforehand**. Knowing where to
  park and which door is unlocked at 7:45 a.m. removes a real source of
  test-morning stress.
- A center you know — your own school, if it hosts — is worth a small
  inconvenience elsewhere.

## What to bring

### Both tests

- **Admission ticket**, printed or on your phone as instructed
- **Photo ID** — the name must match your registration exactly
- **Pencils** (No. 2, for ACT bubbling and for SAT scratch work)
- **Approved calculator** plus **fresh batteries**
- **Watch** without an audible alarm and without smart features
- **Snack and water** for the break
- **Layers.** Test rooms are unpredictable and being cold for three hours is a
  real handicap

### SAT specifically

- Fully **charged laptop or tablet** with Bluebook installed and updated, and
  your **charger**
- Complete **exam setup** in Bluebook in the days before — do not leave this to
  test morning
- If borrowing a device from College Board, you must have requested it at least
  30 days in advance

### Not allowed

Phones (must be off and stored), smartwatches, fitness trackers, earbuds,
separate timers, highlighters on some tests, notes, books, and — obviously —
any recording device. Penalties for a phone that makes noise range from a
warning to score cancellation. Turn it fully off, not to vibrate.

## Calculator rules

Both tests permit most graphing and scientific calculators. Both prohibit:

- Calculators with QWERTY keyboards
- Phones, tablets, laptops, and smartwatches used as calculators
- Anything with internet access, a camera, or audio
- Calculators that need to be plugged in
- Calculators that make noise

Check the current approved-model list on the official site if you use anything
unusual. **Bring fresh batteries** — a dead calculator mid-section is a
catastrophe and the proctor cannot help you.

For the SAT, the built-in Desmos calculator is genuinely excellent and worth
learning even if you bring a handheld. See the
[Desmos playbook](sat/math/06-desmos-playbook.md).

## Test-day timeline

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Night before | Lay everything out. Sleep. Do not study. |
| 6:30-7:00 a.m. | Wake, eat real protein, not just sugar |
| 7:30 a.m. | Arrive at the center |
| 8:00 a.m. | Doors typically close — late arrivals are turned away |
| 8:00-8:45 a.m. | Check-in, seating, instructions, setup |
| 8:45 a.m. onward | Testing begins |
| Mid-test | Scheduled break — eat, drink, move, use the restroom |
| ~12:00-1:00 p.m. | Finish |

**Arrive early.** Doors close at a stated time and proctors do not make
exceptions. Traffic on a Saturday morning is not an excuse anyone accepts.

### During the break

Eat something. Drink water. Stand up and walk. Do not discuss the test with
other students — it is both against the rules and psychologically corrosive.
Do not try to look up an answer. Reset and move on.

## After the test

- **Cancelling scores:** both organizations have deadlines and procedures for
  this. Think hard before doing it — students' self-assessments immediately
  after a test are notoriously bad, and a superscoring school ignores a weak
  section anyway. Under score choice you can usually just not send it.
- **Score verification:** if you believe there was a scoring error, both offer
  paid rescoring services. Genuine errors are rare.
- **Getting scores:** check online rather than waiting for mail. Set a calendar
  reminder for the release date rather than refreshing daily.

## A registration checklist

Print this or copy it into your notes.

- [ ] Identified my earliest application **and scholarship** deadline
- [ ] Worked backward to find the latest usable test date for each test
- [ ] Checked whether my schools superscore
- [ ] Checked whether my schools accept self-reported scores
- [ ] Asked my counselor about a fee waiver
- [ ] Started any accommodations request (7+ weeks lead time)
- [ ] Created or recovered my College Board account
- [ ] Created or recovered my MyACT account
- [ ] Uploaded an acceptable photo to both
- [ ] Registered, with a specific center confirmed
- [ ] Added the test date, and the deadline for my *backup* date, to a calendar
- [ ] Installed and set up Bluebook (SAT)
- [ ] Verified my calculator is approved and has fresh batteries
