# How to Use This Guide

Most test prep fails for a boring reason: students read about the test instead
of practicing it, then mistake familiarity for skill. You can read this entire
library in a weekend and gain nothing. The guides are here to make your practice
*sharper*, not to replace it.

This file explains the method. It is short on purpose.

---

## The core loop

Everything in this library runs on one cycle:

```
Diagnose  →  Learn the pattern  →  Drill it cold  →  Review errors  →  Re-drill spaced
```

Each stage has a failure mode. Most students skip straight to "drill" and never
review, which is why their scores plateau after the first ten hours.

### 1. Diagnose

Take a full, timed practice section before you study anything. Not to get a
score — to find out *which skills* are broken. A 620 Math score built on shaky
algebra needs a completely different plan than a 620 built on running out of
time.

Use [Diagnostic routing](plans/diagnostic-routing.md) to turn that result into a
target list. The app's Progress view gives you per-skill accuracy once you have
enough attempts.

### 2. Learn the pattern

Read the specific domain guide for a weak skill. Not the whole library — the one
guide. Each domain guide is structured the same way:

- **What the test actually asks** — the real question forms, not a textbook
  chapter.
- **What you must know cold** — the memorization floor.
- **Method** — a repeatable procedure.
- **Patterns and tells** — how right and wrong answers tend to look here.
- **Traps** — the specific ways this skill is used to punish you.
- **Worked examples** — reasoning shown in full.

### 3. Drill it cold

Filter the app to that exact domain and skill. Do 10-15 items **untimed** first
to build the procedure, then 15-20 items **timed** to build speed.

Untimed practice teaches accuracy. Timed practice teaches selection and triage.
They are different skills and you need both. Doing only timed work locks in your
current bad habits at higher speed.

### 4. Review errors

This is the stage everyone skips, and it is worth more than the drilling.

For every miss, write down which of the four error types it was. See
[Error log and review method](07-error-log-and-review-method.md). The categories
matter because they have completely different fixes:

| Error type | What it means | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Content** | You didn't know the rule or formula | Learn it. Flashcard it. |
| **Process** | You knew it but applied it wrong | Slow down, write more steps |
| **Careless** | You knew it and would get it right on a re-do | Change a mechanical habit |
| **Time** | You would have gotten it with more clock | Triage and pacing work |

A student whose misses are 70% careless does not need more content study. They
need to stop doing arithmetic in their head. That is a one-week fix worth 30-60
points, and no amount of reading about quadratics will find it.

### 5. Re-drill spaced

Come back to the same skill 3 and 10 days later. The app's **Missed** session
mode exists for this. Spacing is not optional — it is the difference between
recognizing a concept and being able to produce it under pressure.

---

## How much practice is enough

Rough guidance, per weak skill:

- **30-40 items** to move a skill from broken to functional.
- **60-80 items** to move it from functional to reliable under time.

The bank holds 575 items per section with at least 175 Easy, 250 Medium, and 150
Hard, so you will not run out. Do not do easy items to feel good; do them only
to build a procedure, then move up.

**Difficulty selection rule:** practice at the level where you get roughly
60-75% correct. Below 50% you are guessing and learning nothing. Above 85% you
are rehearsing what you already know.

## Session design

A good study session is 45-75 minutes and looks like this:

| Minutes | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0-5 | Warm-up: 5 easy items in a skill you already have |
| 5-15 | Read or re-read one guide section |
| 15-40 | Drill 15-25 items in that skill |
| 40-60 | Review **every** item you missed, and every item you got right but were unsure about |
| 60-70 | Missed-items session from previous weeks |

That last row is the one people cut when they're tired. Cut the drilling
instead — reviewing 10 items properly beats attempting 40 carelessly.

### Reviewing items you got *right*

Flag any item where you were unsure, guessed, or took too long even though you
landed on the credited answer. A right answer for the wrong reason is a future
wrong answer. The app's flag feature is for exactly this.

## Full-length practice tests

Take them, but not too many and not too early.

- **Minimum 3** before test day, **maximum about 6**. Beyond that you are
  burning material and energy for diminishing returns.
- The first one is a diagnostic. Take it before studying.
- The rest go roughly every 2-3 weeks, and always on a Saturday morning at the
  real start time, in one sitting, with real timing and real breaks.
- Your last full test should be **7-10 days before** the real one, never the day
  before. The final week is for light review and sleep.

Simulating conditions matters more than people think. Score inflation from
taking a test on your couch, in three sittings, with your phone nearby, is real
and it will hurt on test day.

---

## What to do the last week

1. No new material. You cannot learn a new skill in five days, and trying makes
   you anxious.
2. Re-read your error log. This is its whole payoff.
3. Light drilling only — 20-30 items a day, mixed, mostly in skills you're good
   at. You want to walk in feeling fluent.
4. Re-read the relevant [pattern guides](05-universal-answer-patterns.md) the
   day before. These are quick wins that survive short-term memory.
5. Sleep. Two nights before matters more than the night before.

## A note on scores and expectations

Realistic movement from serious, well-run prep:

| Starting point | Realistic gain with 8 weeks of real work |
| --- | --- |
| Low baseline, big content gaps | Large — the gaps are the opportunity |
| Mid-range | Moderate — usually process and pacing, not content |
| Already high | Small — you are fighting for a handful of items |

Gains come disproportionately from fixing careless errors and pacing, not from
mastering the hardest content. The last five questions of a math section are
worth exactly as much as the first five, and the first five are much easier to
secure.

If you are retaking, look at your actual score report first. It tells you which
reporting categories were weak. Start there, not at the beginning of this
library.
