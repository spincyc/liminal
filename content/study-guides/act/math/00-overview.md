# ACT Mathematics — Overview

**45 questions · 50 minutes · 67 seconds per question**

One of the three Composite sections. Four answer choices per question in the
enhanced format — up from the old five, which improves blind-guess odds from 20%
to 25%.

---

## Structure

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Questions | 45, all multiple choice (4 choices) |
| Time | 50 minutes |
| Calculator | Permitted throughout (approved models) |
| Formula sheet | **None provided** |
| Ordering | Roughly easy → hard |
| Content range | Pre-algebra through early precalculus and basic trigonometry |

### The formula sheet difference

**The ACT gives you nothing.** Every area formula, volume formula, and
trigonometric relationship must be memorized. This is the biggest practical
difference from the SAT and it's why the
[formula reference](07-formula-reference.md) matters more here.

## Pacing

67 seconds per question, but the section is ordered easy to hard, so **bank time
early**.

| At | You should be at |
| --- | --- |
| 12 min | Q15 |
| 25 min | Q28 |
| 38 min | Q38 |
| 50 min | Done |

Note the deliberate front-loading: 15 questions in the first 12 minutes, only 7
in the last 12.

**Target times by position:**

| Questions | Target |
| --- | --- |
| 1-15 | 40-50 sec |
| 16-30 | 60-75 sec |
| 31-45 | 90-110 sec |

**If you're at Q15 at 18 minutes, you are in trouble.** Speed up immediately
rather than gradually — the back half needs the time more than the front half
does.

### The 30-second rule

If you've spent 30 seconds and don't know how to *start*, mark it, guess, and
move. Two minutes on one hard question costs you three easy ones.

## Content scope

The ACT covers more topics than the SAT, but less deeply. Things the ACT tests
that the SAT largely doesn't:

- Matrices (basic operations, determinants of 2×2)
- Logarithms
- Complex numbers beyond the basics
- Trigonometric graphs, amplitude and period
- Law of Sines and Law of Cosines
- Conic sections beyond circles
- Vectors (basic)
- Sequences and series
- Permutations and combinations
- More extensive coordinate geometry

None of these is deep. Most appear as one or two questions testing whether you
know a definition or a formula.

## The four techniques that carry the section

### 1. Backsolving

The choices are numbers. Plug them into the problem.

**Start with the second-smallest or second-largest.** If choices are ordered and
yours is too big, you know the direction — you rarely test more than two.

Because **every ACT Math question is multiple choice**, backsolving is available
on all 45 questions. On the SAT, grid-ins remove this option for a quarter of
the section. Use it more aggressively here.

### 2. Plugging in numbers

When the question and choices contain variables, pick concrete values. Avoid
`0` and `1`. Use `100` for percent problems.

### 3. Estimation

Get the rough size and sign before computing. On a speed-limited section, an
estimate that eliminates two choices is often worth more than an exact solution.

### 4. Draw and label

For any geometry question without a diagram, sketch one. For any with a diagram,
label everything you know directly on it. Unlabeled diagrams are where geometry
points disappear.

## Answer choice structure

ACT Math distractors are built from specific errors:

| Source | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Intermediate value | You find `x`; they ask for `x + 3` |
| Sign error | `−7` and `7` both present |
| Reciprocal | `3/4` vs `4/3` |
| Off by one | Counting endpoints |
| Radius/diameter swap | |
| Wrong unit | Answer in inches when feet were asked |
| Percent confusion | Of vs. change vs. remaining |
| Forgot the last step | |

**Your answer appearing among the choices confirms nothing.** Estimate or
substitute back.

---

## Guessing strategy

With four choices, a blind guess is 25%. With one elimination it's 33%.

- **Never leave anything blank.** No penalty for wrong answers.
- Have a **default letter** for panic-guessing.
- **Eliminate by magnitude** first — choices that are obviously the wrong size
  die fast.
- With 60 seconds and 5 questions left, don't attempt one carefully. Spend 10
  seconds each eliminating by magnitude, then default-letter the rest.
