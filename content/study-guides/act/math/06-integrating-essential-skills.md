# ACT Mathematics — Integrating Essential Skills

**Catalog domain:** Integrating Essential Skills
**Skills:** Rates and proportional reasoning · Applied measurement ·
Multi-step applications
**~20% of the section** — about 9 questions, the largest domain

This domain is not about advanced content. It's about applying middle-school and
early-high-school mathematics — rates, percents, proportions, areas — inside
multi-step word problems.

**These are the most commonly missed "easy" questions on the ACT.** The math is
simple; the setup and the number of steps are what get people.

---

## Rates and proportional reasoning

### Proportions

```
a/b = c/d   →   ad = bc
```

**Setup discipline:** keep like units in like positions. If the left side is
`miles/hours`, the right side must be too.

### Rates

```
distance = rate × time
work     = rate × time
cost     = rate × quantity
```

**Unit rate:** divide to get "per one." Comparing unit rates is how "which is
the better deal" questions are answered.

### Average speed

**Not the average of the speeds.** Total distance over total time.

> *60 miles at 30 mph, then 60 miles at 60 mph.*
> Times: 2 h and 1 h. Total: 120 miles in 3 hours = **40 mph**, not 45.

This is one of the most reliably tested traps in the domain.

### Combined work

Rates add.

> *A fills a tank in 6 hours, B in 3 hours. Together?*
> `1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2` of the tank per hour → **2 hours**.

**The general form:** if it takes `a` and `b` alone, together it takes
`ab/(a + b)`.

### Direct and inverse variation

| Type | Relationship | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct** | `y = kx` | Doubling `x` doubles `y` |
| **Inverse** | `y = k/x`, i.e. `xy = k` | Doubling `x` halves `y` |

**Method:** find `k` from the given pair, then use it.

> *`y` varies inversely with `x`. When `x = 4`, `y = 9`. Find `y` when `x = 6`.*
>
> `k = xy = 36`. Then `y = 36/6 = 6`.

**Joint and combined variation** occasionally appear: `y = kxz` (joint), or
`y = kx/z`.

### Scale and maps

A scale of `1 : 500` means one unit on the drawing is 500 in reality. Set up a
proportion.

**Area on a scale drawing scales by the square of the linear factor.** A map at
1:100 has areas 1:10,000.

## Percentages

The highest-error topic in the domain.

### The three forms

| Question | Setup |
| --- | --- |
| What is `p%` of `n`? | `(p/100) × n` |
| `a` is what percent of `b`? | `(a/b) × 100` |
| `a` is `p%` of what? | `a ÷ (p/100)` |

### Percent change

```
percent change = (new − old)/old × 100
```

**The denominator is always the ORIGINAL.** This is the single most common
percent error on the test.

### Multipliers

| Change | Multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| +15% | `× 1.15` |
| −15% | `× 0.85` |
| +100% | `× 2` |
| −40% | `× 0.60` |

Using multipliers instead of computing-and-adding is faster and far less
error-prone. Chain them for successive changes.

### Successive changes don't add

> +20% then −20%: `1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96` → a **4% decrease**.

### Vocabulary

| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| increased **by** 30% | `× 1.30` |
| increased **to** 30% of | `× 0.30` — a decrease |
| 30% **more than** x | `1.30x` |
| 30% **less than** x | `0.70x` |
| **percent** increase | relative to original |
| **percentage point** increase | absolute difference between percents |

Going from 20% to 25% is **5 percentage points** but a **25 percent** increase.

### Applications

**Discount:** `final = original × (1 − discount)`
**Tax/tip:** `final = subtotal × (1 + rate)`
**Successive discounts:** multiply the factors — a 20% then 10% discount is
`0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72`, a 28% total discount, not 30%.
**Simple interest:** `I = Prt`
**Compound interest:** `A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)`
**Markup vs. margin:** markup is on cost, margin is on price. Read which.

## Applied measurement

### Unit conversion

Dimensional analysis: write units as fractions and cancel.

> *Convert 45 mi/h to ft/s:*
> ```
> 45 mi   5280 ft    1 h
> ----- × -------- × ------ = 66 ft/s
>  1 h      1 mi     3600 s
> ```

**If the units don't cancel to what you want, the setup is wrong.** This check
is the whole value of writing them out.

### Area and volume unit conversion

**Square the factor for area, cube it for volume.**

> `1 ft² = 144 in²` (because `12² = 144`), not 12.
> `1 yd³ = 27 ft³` (because `3³ = 27`).

A recurring ACT trap.

### Composite figures

Break into known shapes. Shaded regions are usually "big shape minus small
shape."

### Common applied setups

| Problem type | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Fencing / perimeter | Perimeter, watch for missing sides |
| Flooring / painting | Area; watch for windows and doors to subtract |
| Filling a container | Volume, then divide by a rate |
| Cost per unit area | Area × price per unit |
| Number of tiles needed | Total area ÷ tile area, then **round up** |

**"How many are needed" always rounds up.** You can't buy 12.3 boxes of tile.
Conversely, "how many can you make" rounds **down**.

## Multi-step applications

The defining feature of this domain: questions requiring three or more
operations.

### Method

1. **Read the entire question before computing anything.** Know what you're
   solving for.
2. **Write down what's given** with units.
3. **Circle what's asked**, with its units.
4. **Plan the steps** before executing. Two seconds of planning prevents solving
   for the wrong thing.
5. Execute, writing each step.
6. **Check that your answer has the right unit and a plausible magnitude.**

### Where these go wrong

| Failure | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Solved for an intermediate value | Circle the target first |
| Lost a unit conversion mid-problem | Write units at every step |
| Did two steps mentally, made an error | Write every step |
| Answered a different question | Reread the last line before selecting |
| Rounded too early | Keep precision until the final step |

**The intermediate-value trap is the most common.** A three-step problem gives
item writers three natural wrong answers — the results after step one and step
two. Both will be in the choices.

---

## Patterns and tells

**Estimate first.** On a multi-step problem, a rough estimate catches setup
errors before you invest 90 seconds.

**Backsolve when the choices are numbers** and the setup is complicated. Working
backward from an answer is often easier than building the equation.

**Write the units.** In this domain specifically, unit tracking prevents more
errors than any other habit.

**Check plausibility.** If a question asks for the number of people and you get
14.7, you made an error or need to round — decide which deliberately.

**Watch the rounding direction.** "At least," "needed," "to ensure" → round up.
"At most," "can be made," "fit" → round down.

---

## Traps

| Trap | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Intermediate value** | Answered after step two of three |
| **Percent change denominator** | Used the new value |
| **Successive percents added** | +20% then −20% ≠ 0 |
| **Averaged the speeds** | Instead of total/total |
| **Area unit conversion** | Didn't square the factor |
| **Rounded the wrong way** | Down when you needed up |
| **Rounded too early** | Compounded error |
| **Missed a unit conversion** | Mixed minutes and hours |

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — Multi-step with units

> *A printer produces 18 pages per minute. How many pages does it produce in 2
> hours and 15 minutes?*

Convert: `2 h 15 min = 135 minutes`.
`18 × 135 = 2430` pages.

Trap choices: `18 × 2.25 = 40.5` (used hours), `18 × 15 = 270` (used only the
minutes).

### Example 2 — Successive percent

> *A jacket costs $80. It is marked down 25%, then an additional 10% off the sale
> price. What is the final price?*

`80 × 0.75 × 0.90 = 80 × 0.675 = $54`.

Trap: `80 × 0.65 = $52` (added the discounts).

### Example 3 — Rounding direction

> *Tiles are 8 inches square. How many are needed to cover a floor 10 feet by
> 12 feet?*

Floor: `120 in × 144 in = 17,280 in²`. Tile: `64 in²`.
`17,280 / 64 = 270` tiles exactly.

Note the unit conversion: 10 ft = 120 in, 12 ft = 144 in. Using feet directly
(`10 × 12 = 120 ft²`) and dividing by 64 would be nonsense — the units don't
match.

### Example 4 — Inverse variation

> *The time to complete a job varies inversely with the number of workers. If 6
> workers take 10 days, how long do 15 workers take?*

`k = 6 × 10 = 60`. `t = 60/15 = 4` days.

## Drill plan

| Stage | Filter | Volume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Percents | Rates and proportional reasoning, Easy → Medium | 30. **Use multipliers only.** |
| 2. Rates | Rates and proportional reasoning, Medium | 25. Write units at every step. |
| 3. Variation | Rates and proportional reasoning, Medium | 15. Find `k` first, every time. |
| 4. Measurement | Applied measurement, Easy → Medium | 25, including area/volume conversions |
| 5. Multi-step | Multi-step applications, Medium → Hard | 30. **Circle the target before computing.** |
| 6. Mixed timed | Whole domain | 30 at 65 sec each |

This is the largest ACT Math domain and the most improvable, because the losses
are procedural rather than conceptual. If your error log shows careless and
process errors clustered here, stages 1, 2, and 5 with their written-out habits
are the highest-value work available to you on the whole section.
