# Test Formats and Scoring

What each test actually is, as of the 2026-27 testing year. Both tests changed
substantially in recent years, and a lot of advice floating around online
describes formats that no longer exist. If a resource mentions a 5-choice ACT
Math section, a required ACT Science section, or a paper SAT with a no-calculator
module, it is out of date.

---

## The digital SAT

### Structure

| Section | Modules | Questions | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reading and Writing | 2 | 27 each (54 total) | 32 min each (64 total) |
| *Break* | — | — | 10 min |
| Math | 2 | 22 each (44 total) | 35 min each (70 total) |
| **Total** | **4** | **98** | **2 h 14 min** |

The test is taken on a computer in the Bluebook application, either on your own
device or one you borrow from the test center. Borrowing requires registering at
least 30 days ahead.

### Adaptivity — what it actually means

The SAT is **multistage adaptive at the module level**, not question by
question.

1. You take Module 1. Everyone gets the same mix of easy, medium, and hard.
2. Based on your Module 1 performance, you are routed to either a harder or an
   easier Module 2.
3. Your score is computed from performance across both modules, taking into
   account which Module 2 you received.

Consequences that actually matter for strategy:

- **You cannot reach the top of the scale from the lower-difficulty Module 2.**
  Module 1 therefore carries outsized weight. Do not "warm up" through it.
- **Within a module, you can move freely.** Skip, flag, come back, change
  answers. Adaptivity happens *between* modules, never inside one.
- **Getting a hard Module 2 is good news.** Students routinely panic when
  Module 2 feels brutal. It means you did well.
- **Reading and Writing adapts independently of Math.** A weak RW Module 1 does
  not affect your Math routing.

### Scoring

- Each section scores **200-800**. Total **400-1600**.
- There is **no penalty for wrong answers**. Never leave anything blank.
- Roughly **4 questions per section are unscored pretest items** being trialed
  for future tests. You cannot identify them. This is one reason a single
  brutal-seeming question should never rattle you.
- Raw-to-scaled conversion varies by form. There is no fixed "X questions wrong
  equals Y score" table, though the relationship is stable enough that practice
  test estimates are usually within about 20-30 points.

### Reading and Writing specifics

- Every question has its **own short passage** — typically 25-150 words. There
  are no long shared passages.
- Questions are **ordered by domain, then roughly by difficulty within domain**:
  Craft and Structure first, then Information and Ideas, then Standard English
  Conventions, then Expression of Ideas. Knowing this order lets you predict
  what is coming and manage energy.
- One or two questions per module involve a **paired passage** (Cross-Text
  Connections), and a few involve **tables or graphs** (quantitative Command of
  Evidence).

### Math specifics

- **Calculator permitted throughout.** There is a built-in Desmos graphing
  calculator, and you may also bring an approved handheld. See the
  [Desmos playbook](sat/math/06-desmos-playbook.md) — used well, it converts a
  large fraction of algebra questions into graph-reading questions.
- About **75% multiple choice, 25% student-produced response** ("grid-ins").
- A **reference sheet** with common geometry formulas is provided on screen.
  It is short. See [Formula reference](sat/math/05-formula-reference.md) for
  what is *not* on it and must be memorized.
- Questions within each module run **roughly easy to hard**. The last few
  questions of a module are the hardest ones you will see at that difficulty
  tier.

### Grid-in rules — memorize these

Losing a point to a formatting error is the most avoidable mistake on the test.

- Up to **5 characters** for a positive answer, **6** including a negative sign.
- **No commas, no percent signs, no dollar signs, no units.**
- Fractions and decimals both accepted. Improper fractions are fine; mixed
  numbers are **not** — `3 1/2` will be read as `31/2`. Enter `7/2` or `3.5`.
- Repeating decimals must **fill the grid**, either truncated or rounded:
  `2/3` → `.6666` or `.6667`, not `.67`.
- If a question has multiple valid answers, enter **one**.
- Negative answers are permitted on the SAT (unlike some older formats).

## The enhanced ACT

### Structure

| Section | Questions | Time | Required? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| English | 50 | 35 min | Required |
| Mathematics | 45 | 50 min | Required |
| Reading | 36 | 40 min | Required |
| Science | 40 | 40 min | **Optional** |
| Writing (essay) | 1 prompt | 40 min | **Optional** |

Required core: **131 questions, 2 h 5 min**. With Science: 171 questions.

Available on paper and, at many centers, on computer. The content is the same.

### What changed in the enhanced format

If you took the ACT before the redesign, four things are different:

1. **Science is optional** and no longer part of the Composite.
2. **Math has four answer choices**, not five. This meaningfully improves blind
   guessing odds — 25% instead of 20%.
3. **The test is shorter** by roughly 44 questions, with more time per question
   in most sections.
4. **Reading passages are somewhat shorter**, with fewer questions each.

### Scoring

- Each section scores **1-36**.
- **Composite = the average of English, Mathematics, and Reading**, rounded to
  the nearest whole number. Science is *not* in it.
- **Science**, if taken, gets its own 1-36 score and combines with Math into a
  **STEM score**.
- **Writing**, if taken, gets a **2-12** score from four rubric domains and
  combines with English and Reading into an **ELA score**.
- **No penalty for wrong answers.** Never leave anything blank.

### Should you take Science and Writing?

**Science: yes, in almost all cases.** It costs a few dollars extra. Some
colleges and many scholarship and honors programs still want a Science score,
some state systems and NCAA processes reference it, and a good Science score is
a free credential. The only reason to skip it is if Science is a genuine
liability *and* you have confirmed no school on your list wants it — a check
worth doing rather than assuming.

**Writing: check your specific list.** Most colleges no longer require it, but a
handful still do, and requirements change. It costs meaningfully more and adds
40 minutes to an already long morning. If even one school on your list requires
or recommends it, take it — retaking solely for the essay is a waste of a
Saturday.

### Pacing reality

This is where the ACT punishes people. Time per question:

| Section | Time per question |
| --- | --- |
| English | 42 seconds |
| Mathematics | 67 seconds |
| Reading | 67 seconds |
| Science | 60 seconds |

The ACT is a speed test in a way the SAT is not. Content difficulty is generally
lower; the pressure comes from the clock. See
[Timing and pacing](06-timing-and-pacing.md).

## Score reporting, superscoring, and score choice

### How scores reach colleges

- **SAT:** you may send scores to colleges. College Board's Score Choice lets
  you choose which *test dates* to send. Some colleges require all scores —
  check each school's policy.
- **ACT:** you send scores by test date. ACT does not send a full history
  automatically; you select the date.

### Superscoring

Many colleges **superscore**: they take your best section scores across multiple
test dates and combine them.

- **SAT superscore:** best RW + best Math, even from different dates.
- **ACT superscore:** ACT itself calculates a superscore Composite from your
  best English, Math, and Reading across dates.

This is the single strongest argument for taking a test twice. If a school
superscores, a second sitting can only help you — a section that goes badly
simply doesn't count.

**But:** superscore policies vary and not every school does it. Check each
school before assuming.

### Self-reported scores

Many colleges now let you self-report scores on the application and only require
an official report if you enroll. This matters for deadlines: if a school
accepts self-reported scores, a test date whose official report arrives after
the deadline may still work, because you can type in the score yourself as soon
as you see it online. Confirm per school — do not assume.

### Score release timing

| Test | Typical release |
| --- | --- |
| SAT | About 2 weeks after test day |
| ACT | 2-4 weeks after test day; multiple-choice scores often come first, with Writing later |

Build your calendar off the **late** end of these ranges, not the early end. See
[Registration and test day](02-registration-and-test-day.md) for how to work
backward from an application deadline.

## Quick comparison

| | Digital SAT | Enhanced ACT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total required time | 2 h 14 min | 2 h 5 min |
| Score scale | 400-1600 | 1-36 composite |
| Adaptive | Yes, between modules | No |
| Calculator | Entire Math section, Desmos built in | Entire Math section, approved handheld |
| Science section | None | Optional, separate score |
| Essay | None | Optional |
| Time pressure | Moderate | High |
| Reading load | Short discrete passages | Longer passages |
| Math answer choices | 4 (plus grid-ins) | 4 |
| Formula sheet | Provided | **Not provided** |

That last row surprises people. The ACT gives you nothing — every area, volume,
and trigonometry formula must be in your head. See
[ACT formula reference](act/math/07-formula-reference.md).
