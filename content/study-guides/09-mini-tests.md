# Mini Tests

A mini test is 20 questions under a real clock, answered with no feedback, then
reviewed in full. It sits between drilling (one skill, immediate explanations)
and a full practice test (three hours, once every two to three weeks).

Use it when you want a diagnostic signal without losing a Saturday.

---

## What each one contains

| Mini test | Questions | Budget | Composition |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **SAT** | 20 | 28 min | 11 Reading and Writing, 9 Math |
| **ACT** | 20 | 20 min | 8 English, 7 Mathematics, 5 Reading |
| **ACT with Science** | 20 | 20 min | 6 English, 5 Math, 4 Reading, 5 Science |

The section splits mirror each real test's weighting — the SAT's 54/44 and the
ACT's 50/45/36 Composite — so the mix of what you face is representative even
though the length is not.

Difficulty is drawn roughly 30% Easy, 45% Medium, 25% Hard.

Time budgets come from official per-question pacing:

| Section | Seconds per question |
| --- | ---: |
| SAT Reading and Writing | 71 |
| SAT Math | 95 |
| ACT English | 42 |
| ACT Mathematics | 67 |
| ACT Reading | 67 |
| ACT Science | 60 |

See [Timing and pacing](06-timing-and-pacing.md) for where those come from.

## How it differs from practice mode

| | Practice | Mini test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feedback | After every question | Only at the end |
| Hints | Available | Hidden |
| Running score | Shown | Hidden |
| Clock | None | Counts down, then counts overtime |
| Navigation | Forward only | Jump to any question, forward and back |
| Review | Per question, as you go | Full pass over all 20 afterwards |

Withholding feedback is the point. Immediate explanations are excellent for
learning a skill and useless for measuring one — knowing you got the last three
right changes how you approach the fourth.

---

## How to take one

**Set it up like a real section.** Phone away, one sitting, no notes. The
result is only worth something if the conditions are.

**Use the two-pass method.** The numbered strip above the question is a
navigator: answer what you know on the first pass, mark anything that stalls
you, and come back. This is the same triage the real tests reward. See
[Timing and pacing](06-timing-and-pacing.md).

**Apply the 30-second rule.** No idea how to start after 30 seconds? Answer
something, move on, come back if there's time.

**Never leave a blank.** Neither test penalizes a wrong answer. The report
counts blanks as incorrect and will say so.

**Watch the clock, don't obey it.** The timer runs past zero into overtime
rather than cutting you off, so a slow run still finishes and the report tells
you the overage. Finishing 6 minutes late is useful information, not a failure.

---

## Reading the report

You get accuracy overall, by section, and by domain with the weakest first,
plus a note on your pace.

**What it is not:** a scaled SAT total or an ACT Composite. Twenty items is far
too small a sample to estimate a score, and the app does not reproduce the
SAT's adaptive routing or either test's scaling. Treat the percentages as a
pointer to what to drill, nothing more.

**What to do with it:**

1. **Look at the weakest domain**, not the weakest section. "Math 5/9" doesn't
   tell you anything actionable; "Advanced Math 1/4" does.
2. **Check the pace note.** If you ran over the budget, pacing is the
   constraint to fix before content.
3. **Review all 20** — including the ones you got right. A right answer you
   guessed at is a future wrong answer.
4. **Classify each miss** into the four error types from
   [Error log and review method](07-error-log-and-review-method.md). This is
   the step that turns a score into a plan.
5. **Run [diagnostic routing](plans/diagnostic-routing.md)** if the pattern
   surprises you.

## Where it fits in a study plan

| Use | Cadence |
| --- | --- |
| **Opening diagnostic**, before a full test | Once, at the start |
| **Weekly check-in** during content phases | Every 1-2 weeks |
| **Format switch** when alternating SAT and ACT prep | Before each block |
| **Taper week warm-up** | Once, early in the final week |

Do not substitute mini tests for full practice tests. Twenty questions cannot
measure stamina, and stamina is a real component of both tests. The plans in
[`plans/`](plans/) call for three to six full tests; mini tests fill the gaps
between them.

Do not take one every day either. It stops being a measurement and becomes
low-quality drilling — you get the cost of no feedback without the benefit of
targeted practice.

---

## A note on the sample size

Twenty questions is a small sample. Expect noise: two mini tests taken an hour
apart can differ by a wide margin purely by chance, especially at the domain
level where a "domain" may be three or four items.

Read it as a **direction**, not a measurement. If Advanced Math comes up weak
twice in a row, believe it. If it comes up weak once and strong the next time,
you've learned nothing except that four questions is four questions.
