# Official Test Structure and Content Map

Verified against primary official sources on 2026-07-29; the SAT section
structure, module counts, and domain counts were rechecked on 2026-09-25
against the Assessment Framework and the SAT structure page. The 2026-10-02
cold review rechecked question ordering, passage length and enhanced ACT
specifications. This document
records the public test blueprints used to organize independent original
practice content; it does not reproduce test questions. The Learn pages keep
the student-facing numbers, each with its source and date, in
`content/learn/facts.json`.

## SAT

The digital SAT has two sections:

- **Reading and Writing:** 64 minutes. Each short passage (25–150 words) or
  passage pair has one discrete four-option multiple-choice question.
- **Math:** 70 minutes. About 75% of questions are four-option multiple choice
  and about 25% are student-produced responses. Calculator use is permitted
  throughout the digital Math section using an approved calculator or the
  embedded Desmos calculator.

The framework defines these passage lengths using standardized six-character
words, including spaces and punctuation. A paired passage's combined length
must fit the same range; it is not a separate 150-word allowance for each text.

| Section | Modules | Questions per module | Time per module | Section total |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| Reading and Writing | 2 | 27 (25 scored + 2 unscored pretest) | 32 min | 54 questions (50 scored), 64 min |
| Math | 2 | 22 (20 scored + 2 unscored pretest) | 35 min | 44 questions (40 scored), 70 min |

A 10-minute break separates the sections; total testing time is 2 hours 14
minutes. Each section is two-stage adaptive: Module 1 is a broad mix of easy,
medium, and hard questions, and Module 2 is of higher or lower average
difficulty depending on Module 1 performance. College Board does not publish
the routing rule. Students navigate freely within a module but cannot return
to an earlier module.

Reading and Writing questions appear by domain in a fixed order in each
module: Craft and Structure, Information and Ideas, Standard English
Conventions, Expression of Ideas. Within each domain except Standard English
Conventions, questions are grouped by skill and ordered easiest to hardest;
Standard English Conventions questions are ordered easiest to hardest
regardless of skill. Math questions in each module run from easiest to
hardest.

### SAT Reading and Writing domains

| Domain | Official share (scored questions per test) | Skills represented in this platform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information and Ideas | ≈26% (12–14) | Central Ideas and Details; Inferences; Command of Evidence (textual and quantitative) |
| Craft and Structure | ≈28% (13–15) | Words in Context; Text Structure and Purpose; Cross-Text Connections |
| Expression of Ideas | ≈20% (8–12) | Rhetorical Synthesis; Transitions |
| Standard English Conventions | ≈26% (11–15) | Boundaries; Form, Structure, and Sense |

The official Form, Structure, and Sense testing points are subject-verb
agreement, pronoun-antecedent agreement, verb finiteness, verb tense and
aspect, subject-modifier placement, and genitives and plurals. The catalog's
"parallel structure" subskill is not an official testing point, and genitives
and plurals have no subskill of their own yet.

### SAT Math domains

| Domain | Official share (scored questions per test) | Skills represented in this platform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Algebra | ≈35% (13–15) | Linear equations; linear functions; two-variable equations; systems; inequalities |
| Advanced Math | ≈35% (13–15) | Equivalent expressions; nonlinear equations and systems; nonlinear functions |
| Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | ≈15% (5–7) | Ratios/rates/units; percentages; one- and two-variable data; probability; inference; study design |
| Geometry and Trigonometry | ≈15% (5–7) | Area/volume; lines/angles/triangles; right-triangle trigonometry; circles (including radians and unit-circle trigonometry) |

The practice app uses difficulty labels for transparent study filtering. It
does not claim to implement College Board's multistage adaptive routing or
scaled scoring.

## ACT

The enhanced ACT has three required multiple-choice sections and two optional
sections:

| Section | Current format | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English | 50 questions, 35 minutes | Required |
| Mathematics | 45 questions, 50 minutes | Required |
| Reading | 36 questions, 40 minutes | Required |
| Science | 40 questions, 40 minutes | Optional |
| Writing | 1 essay, 40 minutes | Optional |

The enhanced design includes embedded field-test questions in those totals.
Reading has 27 scored questions and 9 field-test questions; Science has 34
scored questions and 6 field-test questions. Reading's three scored passage
sets may include zero or one paired-text set. Science includes 5–8 scored
questions requiring background knowledge together with passage reasoning.
Liminal's rebuilt Science bank has 80 original questions in 14 passage sets.
Its full Science forms use seven complete sets: two Data Representation sets
with five questions each, four Research Summaries sets with six each, and one
Conflicting Viewpoints set with six. All 40 answers count for practice; Liminal
does not designate unscored field-test questions or predict a scaled score.

Rechecked on 2026-10-03 against Table 2.4 of ACT's February 2026 Design
Framework: the official design uses six operational passages (two Data
Representation, three Research Summaries, one Conflicting Viewpoints) and a
separate six-question field-test passage. The extra Research Summaries set in
Liminal is a practice approximation. The finite inventory and its provisional
tiers are not empirical calibration or proof of official-form equivalence.

The current Composite score is based on English, Mathematics, and Reading.
Science receives a separate section score and contributes to a STEM score when
taken. Writing receives a separate score and contributes to an ELA score when
taken.

### ACT reporting categories

| Section | Domains used by this platform |
| --- | --- |
| English | Production of Writing; Knowledge of Language; Conventions of Standard English |
| Mathematics | Preparing for Higher Math (Number and Quantity, Algebra, Functions, Geometry, Statistics and Probability); Integrating Essential Skills; Modeling |
| Reading | Key Ideas and Details; Craft and Structure; Integration of Knowledge and Ideas |
| Science | Interpretation of Data; Scientific Investigation; Evaluating Scientific Arguments and Models with Evidence (the archived bank retains the older Evaluation of Models, Inferences, and Experimental Results label) |
| Writing | Ideas and Analysis; Development and Support; Organization; Language Use and Conventions |

ACT publicly describes Modeling as integrated across Mathematics rather than a
separate mutually exclusive bucket. In this bank, each Math item has one primary
domain and may also carry `modeling` as a format/tag. Writing prompts are
classified by their main planning challenge while every prompt's guide covers
all four rubric domains.
