## Grades 12 and 11: curriculum scope and limits

The six-unit sequence is substantively present: interacting ideas and uncertainty; American literary development across periods; layered language and rhetorical purpose; complete Shakespeare and American plays; foundational civic documents and legal reasoning; and qualified synthesis across literary, historical and technical sources. Grade 12 supplies different complete plays from Grade 11, as its skeleton requires. The Mayflower Compact, Declaration, Preamble, selected Bill of Rights amendments, full Second Inaugural and majority/dissent excerpts supply authentic civic reading. Technical tasks use supplied procedures, measurements, tables and explicitly fictional classroom records; they are not presented as empirical research.

Grade 11 unit 1 remains chiefly a short original-text bridge. The Franklin excerpt and historical analysis are authentic/source-grounded, but the unit does not supply the skeleton's sustained complex story and essay. Later complete plays materially improve yearly sustained reading without retroactively filling that unit-level gap. Both grades still use many short originals and short technical simulations; metadata does not establish independent upper-band text complexity or student mastery. Grade 12's longer Hawthorne/Douglass additions resolve the concrete missing-literary/nonfiction-source issue without certifying the entire first unit's complexity target.

Performance scores prescribe concrete classroom interpretations, with directions to perform or record them before making observations. They support text-based comparison, but this reviewer did not observe a live or recorded student performance. No student demonstration of media competency is certified. Historical secondary explanations often depend on the primary selection; agreement between them must not be counted as independent corroboration. The unit-6 dossiers preserve uncertainty and source distinctions, but are not a substitute for independently assessed college-level reading across a broad disciplinary corpus. Neither full ELA coverage nor complete RL/RI/RH/RST mastery is claimed.
