  negative factors, cleared denominators, and decimal coefficients. A Python
  `fractions.Fraction` calculation substituted each unique equation solution
  back into the reduced equation exactly. The reported steps were then read
  against the original prompts and those reductions.
- Checked both accounts at the savings intersection, all four rectangle
  sides, both printing costs at the intersection and the next integer pack,
  and the distinction between algebraic real solutions and physically allowed
  item counts. The pieces model explicitly counts items and labels together;
  its additions therefore have coherent units.
- Checked rate differences and elapsed-time multiplication, slope signs and
  reduced fractions, intercepts, proportionality, and the completeness of
  open-response instructions. Text instructions and coordinates supply the
  information needed to answer the sampled questions.
- Checked every student graph's positive intervals, labeled axes, coordinate
  bounds, plotted points, and supplied line segments. Exact coordinates in
  hose/intercept labels support readings that fall between five-liter grid
  lines. The slope and equation-from-graph points fall on integer grid lines.
- Checked all six answer-only graphs against the blind equations: every
  supplied point lies on its equation and inside its grid, and every line
  endpoint satisfies the equation. Some line endpoints lie outside the y
  window intentionally so the answer extends across the grid. Inspection of
  `renderGraph` in `src/app/course-render.js` confirmed numeric clipping to
  the graph rectangle; those endpoints are not rejected as invalid points.
- Checked source branches after unblinding. Nonzero coefficient differences
  protect the unique-solution equations; the fractions have positive unequal
  denominators; contextual lengths, rates, counts, and intersection times
  stay positive or nonnegative as appropriate. No additional source defect
  was identified.
