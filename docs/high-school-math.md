# High-school mathematics

The named pathway at `high-school.html` follows **Algebra → Geometry →
Algebra 2 → Trigonometry → Calculus**, with **Conventional sequence · flexible
placement** visible in the interface. It was planned backwards from Calculus
to identify the needed preparation. The learning order remains forward, and
the course names carry no required grade assignment. Each plan budgets 36
instructional weeks, including practice, review, assessment, and reteaching.
Placement depends on prerequisite knowledge and local requirements; this
library does not award school credit or certify mastery.

## Shared courses and transitions

| Named course | Canonical instructional content | Preparation for the next course |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Algebra | Common Core mathematics, suggested Grade 9 | Equations, functions, and coordinates support Geometry's proofs and models. |
| Geometry | Common Core mathematics, suggested Grade 10 | Similarity, radians, triangle ratios, and proof support Algebra 2. |
| Algebra 2 | Common Core mathematics, suggested Grade 11 | Function families and introductory periodic models support Trigonometry. |
| Trigonometry | New named course, including precalculus foundations | Exact values, identities, inverse restrictions, function synthesis, and difference quotients prepare for Calculus. |
| Calculus | New named course in one real variable | Limits, derivatives, accumulation, and model analysis prepare for further study selected by the learner's goals. |

The first three entries are named views of existing content. Their units,
weekly explanations, questions, and keys have one authored source. A change
to that source reaches both views. Their original standards references remain
visible; the original suggested grade remains source metadata rather than a
placement mandate for the named sequence. The named view supplies its own
course title, prerequisites, and next-course transition. The weekly alias
adapts course identity, title, and scope metadata, and replaces only Week 36's
`connection.after` with that named plan's `nextStep`. Every question, worked
model, answer, and other instructional field stays sourced from the canonical
course; the adaptation never mutates the Common Core file.

Algebra 2 already introduces radians, sine and cosine on the unit circle,
periodic models, and the Pythagorean identity. Its functions unit explicitly
reserves full rational-graph and inverse-composition work for further study.
Trigonometry therefore begins with composition, domains, inverses, and
exponential/logarithmic algebra; develops the trigonometry more deeply; and
returns to polynomial, rational, exponential, and logarithmic functions before
Calculus. This bridge does not assume that the optional Common Core Grade 12
course has been completed.

The official [Common Core trigonometric-functions page](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/HSF/TF/)
distinguishes its core circular-function and modeling content from several
`(+)` extensions, including inverse functions and angle formulas. The new
Trigonometry course draws on that broader subject matter. Its `TRIG.*`
references, and every `CALC.*` reference, are Liminal editorial objectives,
not official Common Core standard identifiers.

## Calculus planned first

Calculus requires students to handle function notation and domains, manipulate
expressions without losing restrictions, compose and invert functions, use
radians and trigonometric identities, and interpret average rates. Its first
three weeks consolidate these prerequisites. That review is not a replacement
for an unfinished algebra or trigonometry course.

This is an introductory single-variable differential and integral calculus
course. It includes the Fundamental Theorem, elementary substitution,
geometric and motion applications, slope fields, separable equations, and
Euler estimates. It does not claim full coverage of either OpenStax calculus
volume. Infinite series, multivariable calculus, comprehensive advanced
integration methods, and formal epsilon-delta proof are outside the core
plan. It is not designated AP Calculus AB or BC and makes no claim of exam
equivalence or college credit.

Each unit lasts three weeks. Weekly targets below specify the planned
progression; the authored lessons provide the actual instruction and tasks.

| Unit / objective | Weeks | Title | Weekly progression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `u1` / `CALC.1` | 1–3 | Function readiness and rates | Domains, algebra, and piecewise rules; composition and inverse/function-family readiness; secants and difference quotients. |
| `u2` / `CALC.2` | 4–6 | Limits and continuity | Numerical, graphical, and one-sided limits; algebraic and infinite limits; continuity and the Intermediate Value Theorem. |
| `u3` / `CALC.3` | 7–9 | Derivative definition and basic rules | Difference-quotient definition; power/sum rules and tangent lines; motion, units, and nondifferentiability. |
| `u4` / `CALC.4` | 10–12 | Product, quotient, and chain rules | Products and quotients; compositions; mixed structures and checks. |
| `u5` / `CALC.5` | 13–15 | Implicit and transcendental differentiation | Trigonometric and exponential derivatives; logarithmic and basic inverse-trigonometric derivatives; implicit differentiation. |
| `u6` / `CALC.6` | 16–18 | Derivatives and function behavior | Critical numbers and extrema; Mean Value Theorem and monotonicity; concavity and coordinated derivative graphs. |
| `u7` / `CALC.7` | 19–21 | Optimization and related rates | Constrained optimization; related rates; linearization, differentials, and approximation limits. |
| `u8` / `CALC.8` | 22–24 | Accumulation and definite integrals | Summation and Riemann sums; signed integrals and properties; numerical approximations and justified error direction. |

The main topic reference is [OpenStax Calculus Volume 1](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/1-introduction),
using selected topics from chapters 1–6. The [derivative-applications outline](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/4-introduction)
and [integration outline](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/5-introduction)
support the topic relationships; their chapter order does not dictate this
36-week schedule. [Volume 2, section 3.6](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-2/pages/3-6-numerical-integration)
supports the numerical-integration scope, and [Volume 2, sections 4.1–4.3](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-2/pages/4-introduction)
support the introductory differential-equations scope. These are references
for concepts and sequencing, not sources of copied examples or exercises.

## Trigonometry and the precalculus bridge

Trigonometry follows Algebra 2 and assumes earlier Geometry. Each of its nine
units lasts four weeks. Revisiting earlier topics uses new tasks and deeper
reasoning, including domains, inverse restrictions, proof, ambiguity, and
model limitations. The last eight weeks deliberately consolidate the function
knowledge that Calculus will need.

| Unit / objective | Weeks | Title | Weekly progression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `u1` / `TRIG.1` | 1–4 | Functions and algebra readiness | Domains and piecewise rules; composition and inverses; exponential/logarithmic algebra; transformations and average rates. |
| `u2` / `TRIG.2` | 5–8 | Radians and circular functions | Angle measure, arc length, and sector area; unit-circle sine/cosine; other ratios and their domains; angular and linear speed. |
| `u3` / `TRIG.3` | 9–12 | Right triangles and exact values | Similarity and ratios; special triangles; reference angles and symmetry; indirect measurement and precision. |
| `u4` / `TRIG.4` | 13–16 | Periodic graphs and models | Sine/cosine transformations; other periodic graphs and asymptotes; model construction; fit and model critique. |
| `u5` / `TRIG.5` | 17–20 | Inverse trigonometry and equations | Principal inverse ranges; compositions and restrictions; interval solutions; general solutions and event times. |
| `u6` / `TRIG.6` | 21–24 | Identities and angle formulas | Basic identities; sum/difference formulas; double/half-angle formulas; identity proof versus equation solving. |
| `u7` / `TRIG.7` | 25–28 | Triangles and geometric applications | Law of Sines and SSA ambiguity; Law of Cosines; areas and bearings; geometric-model selection and critique. |
| `u8` / `TRIG.8` | 29–32 | Precalculus function synthesis | Polynomial/rational features; exponential/logarithmic models; composition, inverses, and transformations; function-family comparison. |
| `u9` / `TRIG.9` | 33–36 | Rates, secants, and calculus readiness | Average rates; difference quotients; approaching values and graphical limit intuition; a cumulative readiness investigation. |

The function bridge is informed by [Precalculus 2e, chapter 1](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus-2e/pages/1-introduction-to-functions),
the [polynomial/rational-function outline](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus-2e/pages/3-introduction-to-polynomial-and-rational-functions),
and the [exponential/logarithmic-function outline](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus-2e/pages/4-introduction-to-exponential-and-logarithmic-functions).
The trigonometric scope draws on chapters [5](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus-2e/pages/5-introduction-to-trigonometric-functions),
[6](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus-2e/pages/6-introduction-to-periodic-functions),
and [7](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus-2e/pages/7-introduction-to-trigonometric-identities-and-equations),
plus the triangle sections in [chapter 8](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus-2e/pages/8-introduction-to-further-applications-of-trigonometry).
The final bridge uses introductory rate and approaching-value ideas also found
in [chapter 12](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus-2e/pages/12-introduction-to-calculus).

This selected course does not promise every topic offered under the name
Precalculus. Matrices, full conic classification, polar and parametric methods,
and a separate sequences-and-series unit are outside this plan. Calculus in
this pathway does not assume those omitted topics. Schools and later courses
may set additional requirements.

## Data, authoring, and verification

`content/high-school-math.json` owns the pathway metadata, source registry,
21 editorial objectives, and five named plans. Original courses have `units`;
aliases instead have `source: { trackId, grade }` and do not carry duplicated
units or weekly bodies. The build resolves each alias against its current
canonical source and brings along the source's standards and citations.
Original course records retain the year-plan teaching fields, including
`prerequisites`, `outcomes`, `nextStep`, `yearBridge`, and `routines`.

The two original weekly files live at
`content/weekly/high-school-math/trigonometry.json` and
`content/weekly/high-school-math/calculus.json`. They use
`trackId: "high-school-math"` and `courseId` instead of `grade`, and otherwise
follow the [weekly coursework contract](weekly-coursework.md). Each unit
references its one corresponding editorial objective; weekly references must
resolve within that unit. A separate fresh author owns each new course,
starting with Calculus. Independent sampled review records what was actually
checked and does not establish human editorial approval or measured outcomes.

The plan route is `high-school.html#<courseId>` with an optional `/u<number>`.
Weekly routes are `weeks.html#high-school-math/<courseId>/<week>`. Unit IDs
are local to a course. The course IDs are `algebra`, `geometry`, `algebra-2`,
`trigonometry`, and `calculus`.

Source URLs and topic locators were checked on 2026-10-07. All new teaching
prose, worked examples, and exercises are original and self-contained. The
references identify scope; the site requires no external textbook to solve
its supplied work.

Validation checks the named-course inventory, alias resolution, 36-week unit
budgets, source and objective references, and the weekly structural contract.
The shared weekly checks also cover stable IDs, repeated displayed tasks, and
student exports that exclude keys. Independent review must additionally assess
the mathematical answers, prerequisite transitions, and actual scope; passing
structural checks is not proof of those judgments. Run the relevant weekly
tests and the repository-wide gate before committing, as described in
[weekly coursework](weekly-coursework.md) and the repository guidance.
